# Literature search and evidence appraisal protocol

## 1. Search strategy
Search bibliographic databases and publisher/repository records using combinations of:
- "precipitated silica" AND (gelation OR aggregation OR fragmentation OR consolidation OR syneresis)
- "silica gel" AND (ageing OR aging OR shrinkage OR restructuring OR permeability OR rheology)
- "sodium silicate" AND (precipitation OR gelation OR aggregation OR speciation)
- "silica aggregate" AND (fractal OR SAXS OR SANS OR scattering OR interparticle)
- targeted author/title/DOI searches for known foundational studies.

Record database, exact query, date searched, and screening decision. Search references cited by key papers and later papers citing them (backward/forward citation chasing).

## 2. Inclusion principles
Include peer-reviewed primary studies and authoritative reviews/books where they materially support:
- precipitation chemistry and primary-particle formation;
- aggregation and gelation kinetics/structure;
- fragmentation of precipitated silica gels;
- consolidation and structural reorganisation;
- natural or enforced syneresis;
- structure–property links relevant to wet and dried precipitated silica.

Dissertations may be used to locate methods, data, and publications, but should not be counted as independent evidence when the same results appear in journal articles.

## 3. Exclusion principles
Exclude or relegate to background sources that:
- do not address silica or a transferable physical mechanism;
- provide no traceable bibliographic record;
- are duplicates of a stronger publication without additional evidence;
- make claims unsupported by the accessible data;
- concern a materially different system without an explicit comparative purpose.

## 4. Source-level appraisal
For every candidate source, record:

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Bibliographic verification | Exact title, authors, journal, year, volume/pages/article number, DOI; publisher/Crossref/repository confirmation |
| Material relevance | Precipitated silica, sodium-silicate gel, colloidal silica, TEOS gel, or other? |
| Study type | Experiment, simulation, theory, review, book, dissertation |
| Methods | Are preparation, conditions, measurement methods, and analysis sufficiently described? |
| Evidence | What was directly measured or calculated? What is inferred? |
| Mechanism | Demonstrated, supported, proposed, or speculative? |
| Transferability | What limits extrapolation to industrial precipitated silica? |
| Review use | Which claim/section can this source legitimately support? |
| Limitations | Controls, assumptions, uncertainty, scope, reproducibility, conflicting evidence |

## 5. Provisional labels
- A: direct and strong evidence for the review question.
- B: high-value mechanistic/methodological evidence, but with clear material or transferability limitations.
- C: useful context or indirect evidence.
- D: exclude from the evidential core.
- V: verification incomplete; do not cite as established evidence until resolved.

Scientific quality and relevance are recorded separately. A rigorous TEOS study may be high quality but only B/C for claims about industrial precipitated silica.

## 6. Claim-level evidence control
Every substantive manuscript claim should be linked to one or more source records and tagged internally as:
- **OBS** — direct experimental observation;
- **CALC** — calculation or quantitative analysis;
- **SIM** — numerical simulation;
- **MODEL** — theoretical/model interpretation;
- **REVIEW** — synthesis from a review;
- **HYP** — hypothesis or proposed mechanism.

Do not phrase MODEL/HYP claims as directly observed facts. Where sources disagree, present the disagreement and assess differences in material, preparation, and measurement.

## 7. Reporting
Maintain a record of:
- search date and query;
- number of records found/screened where available;
- inclusion/exclusion reason;
- full-text availability;
- appraisal status and date;
- duplicate/overlapping datasets.

The target of approximately 50 relevant publications is a planning estimate, not a quota. Do not retain weak sources merely to reach a numerical target.
