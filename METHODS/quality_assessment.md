# Quality assessment rubric

Use this rubric for each source. Score each dimension 0–3 and provide a short justification; do not reduce the scientific judgement to a single number.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bibliographic confidence | Unverified/contradictory | Partial metadata | Publisher/repository record | Cross-checked authoritative records |
| Material relevance | Not relevant | Distant analogue | Related silica system | Direct precipitated-silica system |
| Method transparency | Not assessable | Sparse | Adequate | Detailed and reproducible |
| Evidence strength | Unsupported assertion | Indirect/qualitative | Quantitative but limited | Direct, quantitative, well-controlled |
| Mechanistic resolution | None | Speculative | Supported interpretation | Strongly discriminated by evidence |
| Transferability | None | Very limited | Conditional | Directly relevant to target process |

## Mandatory narrative fields
1. Verified citation and DOI.
2. Study type and material/preparation route.
3. What the study actually did.
4. Main results relevant to our question.
5. What the results do **not** establish.
6. Limitations and potential confounders.
7. Overlap with other publications/datasets.
8. Recommended manuscript use and claim tags (OBS/CALC/SIM/MODEL/REVIEW/HYP).
9. Provisional A/B/C/D class, or V if verification remains incomplete.

## Decision rule
A source is not A merely because it is frequently cited, published in a prestigious journal, or has a plausible title. A requires direct relevance and strong evidence for a defined part of the review question. A source can be methodologically excellent but receive B/C because its material system is not industrial precipitated silica.

## Review-level quality control
- Cross-check all citations against authoritative bibliographic records.
- Verify quotations and numerical values against the full text.
- Check whether multiple papers reuse the same experimental dataset.
- Preserve negative, null, and conflicting results.
- Do not claim systematic-review completeness unless the search process supports it.
- Do not claim novelty until existing reviews and recent primary literature have been compared.
