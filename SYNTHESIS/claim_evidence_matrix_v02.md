# Claim–evidence matrix (working version 01)

**Status:** internal synthesis tool; not yet manuscript-ready prose.  
**Updated:** 11 November 2026. Version 02 adds independent evidence from Bałdyga et al. (2012) and Tognonvi et al. (2011, 2019).  
**Purpose:** connect each proposed review claim to the actual observations, models and source families that support it. Evidence strength refers to the claim as narrowly worded below, not to the overall prestige of a source.

## Confidence key

- **High:** direct quantitative observations in a relevant material system; major methodological limitations are understood.
- **Moderate:** direct evidence exists, but it is concentrated in one research family, limited to model formulations, or requires a qualified transfer from a related silica system.
- **Low / provisional:** mostly indirect evidence, model interpretation, abstract-only appraisal, or substantial uncertainty in material-system equivalence.
- **Not established:** current database does not support the claim as stated.

## Research-family dependence and effective independence

1. **Schlomach–Kind / Quarch lineage:** Schlomach (2006), Schlomach & Kind (2004), Quarch (2010), Quarch & Kind (2010 Parts 1–2), and Quarch et al. (2010). These are multiple outputs from a connected programme. Quarch explicitly identifies standard semi-batch runs as repeats of Schlomach. Some Quarch rheology, batch fragmentation and reactor/ultrasound modules are additional experiments, but the full article/thesis figure concordance is still incomplete.
2. **Wilhelm–Kind lineage:** Wilhelm & Kind (2014, 2015) and Wilhelm (2017). The dissertation reproduces published syneresis and pressure-cell results while adding centrifugal tests, pore-liquid analysis and model development.
3. **Wijnen/Beelen lineage:** two 1991 papers on water-glass-derived silica-gel formation/ageing. Treat as related structural outputs, not automatically independent replication.
4. **External independent comparator:** Knoblich & Gerber (2001), water-glass-derived SiO2 sols; Chal et al. (2019), commercial silica powder ageing. These broaden the structural context but do not independently reproduce the industrial wet-gel fragmentation-to-syneresis sequence.

## Priority gaps (ranked)

1. **Independent wet-gel syneresis/consolidation data for acid-precipitated silica:** highest priority. Current quantitative direct evidence is heavily concentrated in the Wilhelm/Kind programme.
2. **Direct multiscale structural measurements during wet-gel consolidation:** SAXS/SANS/USAXS, cryo-TEM or other suitable measurements that connect nanoscale network change to macroscopic shrinkage and liquid expulsion.
3. **Independent validation of process-history effects on fragmentation:** especially gel age, ionic strength and energy dissipation under controlled hydrodynamics.
4. **Link from wet-gel history to dried-product properties:** avoid relying on post-drying BET alone where drying artefacts are possible.
5. **Cross-study harmonisation:** reconcile primary particle size, aggregate/fragment size, fractal dimension, scattering q-window, DLS assumptions, solids basis, pH convention and time origin before pooling values.

## Modelling decision: when LAMMPS would add value

A LAMMPS model is **not currently required** to complete the review. The existing literature already contains particle-scale and continuum-style models, and an unvalidated simulation could add apparent detail without resolving an evidence gap.

A narrowly scoped coarse-grained LAMMPS study could become useful if the review identifies a precise, testable question such as:

> Under what combinations of short-range attraction, repulsive barrier and bond-formation kinetics does a pre-aggregated silica network compact under a specified compressive load, and which structural observables distinguish reversible rearrangement from irreversible contact formation?

Possible model observables: coordination number, radius of gyration, pair-correlation function, cluster fractal dimension over a defined length-scale range, pore/void fraction, contact persistence, stress–strain response and recovery after unloading.

**Essential limitations:** a simple Lennard–Jones or bead-spring model cannot represent aqueous silicate speciation, pH-dependent surface chemistry, silanol condensation, counterion effects or pore-liquid hydrodynamics unless these are explicitly parameterised. The existing Sahabi–Kind particle model is a relevant precedent but uses simplified interactions and omits fluid flow. A LAMMPS exercise should therefore be presented as a mechanistic sensitivity study, not a predictive model of industrial silica, unless validated parameters and independent data are available.

**Go/no-go recommendation:** defer implementation until the literature matrix has been completed and the model question is narrowed. If undertaken, use a minimal coarse-grained network model to test one mechanism and compare trends against already published observations; do not attempt reactive atomistic silica chemistry as a first model.
