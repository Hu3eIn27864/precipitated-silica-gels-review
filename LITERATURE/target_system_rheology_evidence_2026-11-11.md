# Direct target-system rheology evidence: sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid silica

**Audit date:** 2026-11-11  
**Purpose:** identify mechanical/structural evidence relevant to the target precipitated-silica system, without confusing it with alkoxide-derived silica gels or sodium-silicate grouts.

## Evidence hierarchy

### 1. Kaide, Saeki & Kikuchi (2011), sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid silica sols

**Reference:** Kaide, A., Saeki, T. & Kikuchi, S. (2011). The Effect of Controlling the Aging Condition on the Gelling Property of Silica Sols. *Nihon Reoroji Gakkaishi* 39(5), 205–210. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1678/rheology.39.205. Open full text: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/rheology/39/5/39_5_205/_pdf

**Material and methods**
- Silica sols produced by neutralisation of sodium silicate with sulfuric acid in a Y-shaped continuous mixer.
- SiO2 concentration in sodium-silicate feed: 284 g/L; sulfuric-acid feed: 200 g/L; molar Na2O:SiO2 ratio 3.1.
- Product-sol SiO2 concentration for the rheology study: 8 g/100 mL.
- Rheology followed by dynamic viscoelastic measurements (G', G'', phase angle) at 1 Hz and strain 0.1; creep/recovery test on 30 mm cubic gels; instantaneous modulus E0 extracted using a four-component Maxwell/Voigt model. SPM used to inspect gel surface structure.

**Source-reported observations**
- At pH 2, gel time was 40 h at 25 °C and 4.5 h at 50 °C.
- Increasing pH from 2 to 4 sharply shortened gel time; the article also reports a large pH dependence of the instantaneous modulus E0.
- The authors interpret pH 2 networks as more homogeneous, while pH 6 leads to rapid particle coagulation into heterogeneous clusters; pH 4 is intermediate.
- Breaking stress increased during aging, consistent with continuing network formation after the gel point.
- The authors state that heating accelerates the process while not substantially changing the network structure in their tested pH-2 condition; increasing pH affects both gel time and elastic response.

**Use in this project**
- High relevance to target precursor chemistry and direct evidence that aging, pH and temperature affect gelation and mechanical response.
- Supports treating network mechanics as an evolving state, not a fixed constant.
- The reported E0 is an instantaneous creep-model modulus, not automatically the constrained modulus M required by a poroelastic consolidation model. Do not substitute E0 for M without Poisson ratio, drainage condition, constitutive mapping and test geometry.
- The study does not report an independent intrinsic permeability measurement or a syneresis-coupled permeability law.
- Its Y-reactor silica sols are chemically relevant, but not necessarily identical to the semi-batch industrial precipitated silica and fragmented gel fragments in Wilhelm & Kind. Record as direct chemistry / adjacent process route, not exact specimen equivalence.

### 2. Kaide & Saeki (2012), alkaline sol transition

**Reference:** Kaide, A. & Saeki, T. (2012). Rheology of Silica Gel Obtained from Alkaline Sol Transition. *Nihon Reoroji Gakkaishi* 40(4), 165–170. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1678/rheology.40.165

**Source-reported scope**
- Sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid silica sols; alkaline sol transition compared with acid sol.
- Dynamic viscoelastic measurements, creep tests and morphological measurements.
- Authors attribute different gel properties to differences in particle bonding and/or particle size in the network.

**Use**
- Important acid/alkaline comparison for the target precursor chemistry.
- No permeability values established from the accessible abstract; do not infer hydraulic properties from rheology alone.

### 3. Wang, Winter & Auernhammer (2014), reactive precipitated silica gel rheology

**Reference:** Wang, M., Winter, H. H. & Auernhammer, G. K. (2014). Time and frequency dependent rheology of reactive silica gels. *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science* 413, 159–166. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2013.09.035

**Source-reported scope**
- Sodium silicate mixed with dilute sulfuric acid; nanoscale silica particles grow and aggregate into a spanning gel network.
- Focus on high-pH silica solubility and its effect on gel mechanics.
- Storage modulus grows approximately logarithmically with aging time; frequency sweeps reveal low-frequency structural relaxation.
- Authors associate finite silica solubility at high pH with finite bond lifetime and physical-gel-like behaviour.

**Use**
- Strongly relevant to the time-dependent mechanical response and the possibility of reversible/dynamic bonds at high pH.
- Supports a time-dependent/relaxing constitutive description rather than a single elastic modulus.
- Does not independently identify permeability or provide a syneresis permeability law. The gel preparation and chemistry must be checked against the specific Wilhelm & Kind formulations before direct comparison.

### 4. Schlomach & Kind (2004), semi-batch precipitation and aggregation

**Reference:** Schlomach, J. & Kind, M. (2004). Investigations on the semi-batch precipitation of silica. *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science* 277(2), 316–326. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcis.2004.04.051

**Source-reported scope**
- Direct semi-batch precipitation from sodium silicate and sulfuric acid.
- Separates primary-particle formation, aggregation up to the gel point, and post-gel fragmentation/compaction.
- Photon-correlation spectroscopy used to follow aggregation; reported gel-cluster fractal dimension rises from approximately 1.8 initially to approximately 2.4 and then remains roughly constant.

**Use**
- Direct process-history/structure evidence for the precipitation and aggregation stages.
- Does not provide permeability or a direct time-dependent modulus measurement in the abstract.

### 5. Valibeknejad et al. (2026), sodium-silicate hydrogels/grouts — adjacent system

**Reference:** Valibeknejad, M., Sweijen, T., Quodbach, J., Wolthers, M. & Raoof, A. (2026). Sodium silicate hydrogels for reducing aquifer permeability: Physicochemical, mechanical, and aging characterisation. *Construction and Building Materials* 538, 147234. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2026.147234. CC BY full text and replication data are linked from the Utrecht University research portal: https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/sodium-silicate-hydrogels-for-reducing-aquifer-permeability-physi/

**Source-reported observations**
- Four sodium-silicate grout formulations with different hardeners were compared.
- Reported examples include shear strength 7.6 kPa after 4 h and 16.5% syneresis after 7 days for C-gel; stiffness 88 kPa after 7 days for A-gel; low syneresis (<4%) for T-gel.
- Formulation-dependent syneresis, strength, leaching and aging were observed.

**Use**
- Valuable adjacent benchmark for joint measurement of gel mechanics, syneresis and aging, with open replication data.
- Not industrial precipitated silica made by the same sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation route; different hardeners, grout formulations and application context. Do not pool with target-system data or use to calibrate the precipitated-silica model without a transferability argument.

## Main conclusion and missing measurement

The literature now contains direct sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid rheology and aging measurements, including acid/alkaline comparisons. The principal gap is narrower than previously stated: **direct target-chemistry mechanical data exist, but a matched independent intrinsic-permeability measurement on the same precipitated-silica gel state has not yet been established.**

A decisive experiment would measure, on matched batches and aging times:
1. small-amplitude oscillatory G'(t), G''(t) and frequency dependence;
2. creep/recovery or a clearly defined constrained/compressive modulus;
3. permeability under a low pressure gradient while monitoring deformation and syneresis;
4. pore/aggregate structure and liquid chemistry (pH, ionic strength, dissolved silica).

The hydraulic test must account for gel deformation and spontaneous syneresis during the measurement; a Darcy calculation that assumes a rigid specimen may be biased.
