# Source assessment PSG-050 — Birch & Geddes (2000): cluster dynamics and particle syneresis during silica hydrogel polymerisation

## Bibliographic record

Birch, D. J. S.; Geddes, C. D. (2000). “Cluster dynamics, growth and syneresis during silica hydrogel polymerisation.” *Chemical Physics Letters* 320(3–4), 229–236. DOI: [10.1016/S0009-2614(00)00241-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0009-2614(00)00241-4).

## Evidence inspected

The publisher-indexed abstract reports an in-situ fluorescence method combining anisotropy decay of solvated and bound dye molecules, with near-ångström-scale sensitivity to the observed dynamics. The experiment follows silica particles during hydrogel polymerisation after mixing sodium silicate solution and sulfuric acid.

The abstract reports the following sequence:
- primary particles with mean hydrodynamic diameter about 1.5 nm are present within 20 min of mixing;
- clusters develop during siloxane polymerisation, reaching secondary-particle mean diameters up to about 4.5 nm after approximately 30 h;
- the cluster growth rate depends on silicate concentration and time to microgelation, t_g;
- subsequent condensation to about 4 nm occurs within one week, during which particle syneresis is reported to dominate;
- effects of D2O and inorganic salts on particle growth are demonstrated.

These values are abstract-level transcriptions, not a new extraction from the article's figures or raw data. The publisher page could not be opened directly in this session; the abstract and bibliographic metadata were available through indexed search output.

## Relevance to CCFSM

This is a particularly relevant chemistry comparator because the reported precursor route is sodium silicate plus sulfuric acid, closely aligned with the target precipitated-silica chemistry. It supplies a useful nanoscale sequence that can inform the interpretation of post-gelation network evolution:

1. **Primary-particle formation precedes mature network contraction.** A single macroscopic “gelation time” may conceal a sequence of primary-particle formation, aggregation/cluster growth and later condensation.
2. **Microgelation time matters.** The reported dependence of cluster growth on silicate concentration and t_g supports retaining formulation/history variables in any bridge-kinetics model.
3. **Post-gelation syneresis has a nanoscale structural component.** The reported decrease in secondary-particle diameter from about 4.5 nm to about 4 nm is an intraparticle/cluster-scale observation. It must not be equated with bulk sample-volume shrinkage or expelled-liquid volume.

## Modelling implications and limits

- The study motivates separating at least three evolving scales: primary silica particles, secondary clusters/aggregates, and the load-bearing macroscopic network.
- A bridge-density variable may be a useful mesoscale closure, but it is not directly measured by this fluorescence method. It must be related to independently measured structural or mechanical observables.
- The reported particle-size decrease is consistent with ongoing condensation/restructuring; it does not by itself quantify macroscopic permeability, constrained modulus, gel contraction, or exudate flux.
- No universal rate law or transferable CCFSM rate constant should be fitted from the abstract values.
- D2O and salt effects are useful candidate perturbations for mechanism-discriminating experiments, but the exact direction and magnitude should be checked against the full paper before quantitative use.
