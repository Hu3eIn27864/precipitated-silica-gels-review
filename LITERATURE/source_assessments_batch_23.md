# Source assessments — batch 23: colloidal silica hydrosols, in-situ RheoSAXS and TEOS ageing

## Scope and access

This batch adds historical colloidal-silica hydrosol studies and two open-access comparators. The project has no institutional access or budget for pay-per-view. No article was purchased. Where only an abstract is available, no full-text claim is made.

## PSG-050 — Frolov & Shabanova (1987): aggregative stability of silica hydrosols

**Reference:** Yu. G. Frolov and N. A. Shabanova, “Factors of aggregative stability of silica hydrosols,” *Langmuir* 3(5) (1987), 640–644. DOI: 10.1021/la00077a009.

**Verification/access:** ACS publisher metadata confirms title, authors, issue, pages and DOI. The article is paywalled; only an abstract-level summary was inspected, without purchase.

**What the abstract supports:** The study analyses pH effects on sol-formation kinetics, sol properties and surface-layer structure. The accessible summary reports a hydrosol-stability minimum near pH 5.5–6.0. The abstract is truncated, so no additional mechanism is asserted.

**Material/transferability:** Silica hydrosol; exact precursor, concentration, particle grade, ionic composition and stabilizer are not available. It is not demonstrably the same as industrial sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation.

**Role/limits:** Useful *Langmuir* precedent for colloidal silica and pH-dependent stability, but not evidence for industrial precipitation or syneresis. Methods, raw data and uncertainty are unavailable. Class B; tags OBS/HYP.

## PSG-051 — Shabanova, Popov & Frolov (1984): polycondensation and coagulation

**Reference:** N. A. Shabanova, V. V. Popov and Yu. G. Frolov, “Kinetics of polycondensation and coagulation in a silica hydrosol,” *Colloid Journal of the USSR* 46(5) (1984), 986–993.

**Verification/access:** Bibliographic record is listed by EurekaMag and later scholarly references. An abstract-level summary was inspected; original journal text was not obtained.

**What the abstract supports:** The study compares homogeneous polycondensation in aqueous silicic acid with heterophase growth on formed sol particles and coagulation. The summary reports increased heterophase polycondensation above pH 2 despite increased activation energy, a decrease near pH 9.5, and faster coagulation in pH 2–5.5 attributed to disruption of solvation layers as surface silanol groups dissociate.

**Bibliographic correction:** One secondary bibliography misprints the volume as 51. The indexed record and an open 2022 article's reference list both identify volume 46, issue 5, pages 986–993; volume 46 is retained.

**Material/transferability:** Aqueous silicic-acid hydrosol; exact precursor, concentration and electrolyte background remain unverified. This is a mechanistic comparator, not industrial precipitation evidence.

**Role/limits:** Helps distinguish homogeneous polycondensation, surface-mediated particle growth and coagulation. No quantitative rate constants or activation energies are extracted from the abstract. Class B; tags OBS/HYP.

## PSG-052 — Shabanova & Silos (1996): electrolyte-induced sol–gel transition

**Reference:** N. A. Shabanova and I. V. Silos, “Sol-gel transition at coagulation of colloidal silica by electrolytes,” *Colloid Journal of the Russian Academy of Sciences* 58(2) (1996), 256–261 in the English translation; *Kolloidny Zhurnal* 58(2), 266–271 in the Russian original.

**Verification/access:** ResearchGate abstract and methods inspected; EurekaMag and a later Russian-language bibliography support the metadata and dual pagination. Full text was not available in the inspected source.

**What the abstract supports:** Viscometry, turbidimetry, dynamic light scattering and peptization were used. Gelation was associated with large particles/aggregates with radii above approximately 100–200 nm. The authors distinguish percolating gel structures from dispersions unstable with respect to sedimentation and interpret the observations using DLVO concepts.

**Material/transferability:** Colloidal silica hydrosol destabilized by electrolytes; the full electrolyte set and concentrations are not available.

**Role/limits:** Useful comparator for aggregation-to-gel transition and aggregate connectivity. The 100–200 nm scale is system-specific, not a universal gel threshold. Do not transfer it to industrial precipitated silica without matched particle size, ionic strength, concentration and measurement definition. Class B; tags OBS/MODEL.

## PSG-053 — Shabanova et al. (1993): mineral-acid effects on gelation

**Reference:** N. A. Shabanova, I. V. Silos, E. V. Golubeva, E. A. Konovalov and A. P. Zakharov, “Regularities of the influence of mineral acids on the gel-formation kinetics in colloid silica,” *Colloid Journal of the Russian Academy of Sciences* 55(1) (1993), 145–151.

**Verification/access:** Title, author list and pagination are supported by a university repository bibliography. ResearchGate abstract inspected; original full text not located.

**What the abstract supports:** Electrosurface properties and gelation kinetics were studied during mineral-acid addition to colloidal silica. Sulfuric and boric acids had distinct effects. The authors attribute an increase in electrical potential after sulfuric-acid addition to sulfate adsorption and hydroxide ions; boric acid reportedly changes silica electronegativity near neutral and alkaline pH. Surface heteropolycompounds are proposed.

**Material/transferability:** Preformed colloidal silica hydrosol exposed to acid, not simultaneous precipitation from sodium silicate and sulfuric acid.

**Role/limits:** Useful comparator for acid identity, interfacial charge and gelation. Exact pH, concentrations, zeta/electrokinetic values, gel times and uncertainty are unavailable; proposed surface chemistry remains unverified. Class B; tags OBS/HYP.

## PSG-054 — Frolov, Shabanova & Savochkina (1983): electrolyte effects on hydrosol stability and gelation

**Reference:** Yu. G. Frolov, N. A. Shabanova and T. V. Savochkina, “Influence of electrolytes on the stability and gelation of silica hydrosol,” *Colloid Journal* 45(3) (1983), 509–514.

**Verification/access:** Citation found in later scholarly and university repository reference lists. No abstract or original text located.

**Role/limits:** High-priority retrieval lead only. No result, electrolyte identity, direction of effect or mechanism is inferred from the title. Status V; metadata-only.

## PSG-055 — Seljelid et al. (2024): in-situ RheoSAXS of a colloidal silica network

**Reference:** K. K. Seljelid, O. Trigueiro Neto, A. N. Akanno, B. Telli Ceccato, R. P. Ravindranathan, N. Azmi, L. P. Cavalcanti, I. Fjelde, K. D. Knudsen and J. O. Fossum, “Growth kinetics and structure of a colloidal silica-based network: in situ RheoSAXS investigations,” *The European Physical Journal Special Topics* 233 (2024), 2757–2773. DOI: 10.1140/epjs/s11734-024-01250-y.

**Verification/access:** Full open-access Springer article inspected, including methods, figures and results; published 30 August 2024 under CC BY 4.0.

**Material/methods:** Sodium-stabilized commercial Levasil OF50 P, 15 wt% amorphous silica stock with nominal 5 nm particles and pH 9.7–10.5; measured stock zeta potential −48 ± 3 mV at 25 °C. NaCl was the activator. In-situ SAXS and rheology were combined at Diamond Light Source and ESRF. Silica concentration, NaCl concentration, temperature and shear were varied; magnetic microparticles/field were also explored.

**Reported trends:** At 20 °C and 325 mM NaCl, 12 wt% SiO2 gels in roughly 70 min versus roughly 155 min for 9 wt%. Raising NaCl from 325 to 400 mM reduces gel time by about 63% (12 wt%) and 64% (9 wt%). Raising temperature from 20 to 40 °C reduces the 12 wt% gel time to about 30 min. Cluster size approaches about 20 nm (12 wt%) and about 30 nm (9 wt%); the 12 wt% sample measured again after 7 h reaches about 25 nm. Fractal dimensions are close to 2.2. At 1000 s−1 shear, the developing network is disrupted and the sample remains a viscous liquid rather than an arrested gel.

**Role:** Modern in-situ link between processing variables, nanoscale cluster evolution and macroscopic rheology; valuable comparator for aggregation/gelation.

**Limits:** Commercial preformed colloidal silica, not freshly precipitated sodium-silicate/acid silica. No syneresis, consolidation, pore-liquid chemistry or dry-powder properties measured. Measurement windows differ; low-q fitting and time comparisons have limits. Data available on reasonable request. Class B; tags OBS/CALC/MODEL.

## PSG-056 — Maeda et al. (2021): ultrasonic ageing and xerogel pore structure

**Reference:** Y. Maeda, Y. Hayashi, J. Fukushima and H. Takizawa, “Sonochemical effect and pore structure tuning of silica xerogel by ultrasonic irradiation of semi-solid hydrogel,” *Ultrasonics Sonochemistry* 73 (2021), 105476. DOI: 10.1016/j.ultsonch.2021.105476.

**Verification/access:** PubMed metadata and DOI verified; open full text available through PMC7881262. Preparation, characterization, tables and conclusions inspected.

**Material/methods:** TEOS-derived silica hydrogel, HMDSO hydrophobization and ambient-pressure drying. Ageing compared standing versus ultrasound at 100 or 500 kHz, 45 W and 60 °C. BET/N2 adsorption, BJH pore analysis, FE-SEM, FTIR and TG-DTA were used.

**Reported values:** After 6 h ageing, 500 kHz gives pore volume 3.88 cm3/g and average pore diameter 51.6 nm versus 2.97 cm3/g and 22.4 nm for standing ageing. BET areas are similar (649 versus 652 m2/g). For hydrophobization, 500 kHz for 10 min gives 3.50 cm3/g and 40.3 nm versus 2.38 cm3/g and 20.1 nm for standing treatment for 10 min.

**Role:** Shows that post-gel ageing and hydrophobization can affect dry-product pore structure and capillary shrinkage during ambient-pressure drying, even when BET area changes little.

**Limits:** TEOS chemistry differs from industrial precipitated silica. Shrinkage suppression is inferred from apparent density and dry-state pore metrics, not measured as wet-gel syneresis. Prose contains a unit typo for specific surface area (g/cm3); the table correctly reports m2/g. Do not transfer values quantitatively. Class B; tags OBS/CALC/HYP.

## Bibliographic and family audit

### Kontorovich–Ponomareva–Sokolova family

- Ponomareva et al. (1984), hydrogel shrinkage: English translation pages 118–120; Russian original bibliographies give 140–142. Retain both until original issues are inspected.
- Kontorovich et al. (1988), temperature dependence: full six-author list confirmed in an indexed record; pages 1100–1104.
- Kontorovich, Ponomareva and Sokolova (1988), elastic modulus during syneresis: Russian title and bibliographic details confirmed in a university repository reference list; original article unavailable.
- Kontorovich et al. (1991), electrolytes and syneresis: pages 126–128 retained provisionally; one secondary citation gives 126–129.

These sources share investigators and a coherent programme; they are not independent replications. Sample-batch or protocol overlap remains unknown.

### Frolov–Shabanova–Silos hydrosol family

The 1983 electrolyte/gelation, 1984 polycondensation/coagulation, 1987 *Langmuir* stability, 1993 mineral-acid and 1996 electrolyte-induced sol–gel papers share overlapping authors and a sustained hydrosol research programme. Their questions appear complementary, but raw-data overlap is not checked. Treat them as one research family, not five independent replications.

### Independent comparators

Seljelid et al. (2024) use commercial sodium-stabilized silica and RheoSAXS. Maeda et al. (2021) use TEOS-derived hydrogel and focus on ultrasound-assisted ageing/hydrophobization before ambient-pressure drying. Neither is the same material system as industrial precipitated silica.

## Screened but not promoted to the core evidence matrix

Potapov, Cerdan and Gorev (2022), “Silicic Acid Polymerization and SiO2 Nanoparticle Growth in Hydrothermal Solution,” *Polymers* 14(19), 4044, DOI 10.3390/polym14194044, was screened in full text. It is an open-access nucleation/growth model for orthosilicic acid in hydrothermal solution, compared with published dissolved-silica and DLS data; it reports no new industrial precipitation or syneresis experiment. It is not added to the core matrix because its model and material scope are hydrothermal OSA.
