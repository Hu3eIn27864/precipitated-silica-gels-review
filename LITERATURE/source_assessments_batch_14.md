## PSG-037 — Scherer (1989): Mechanics of syneresis I. Theory

**Reference:** G. W. Scherer, “Mechanics of syneresis I. Theory,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 108(1) (1989), 18–27. DOI: 10.1016/0022-3093(89)90328-1.

**Verification and access:** Elsevier publisher record and abstract inspected; title, journal, volume, issue, pages and DOI verified. Full text not accessed.

**Study type and system:** Continuum/mechanical theory for syneresis in silica gels. The abstract defines syneresis as spontaneous gel contraction accompanied by pore-liquid expulsion, even when evaporation is prevented and the gel is immersed in liquid.

**What it supports:** Contraction rate depends on sample size because liquid must flow through small pores against hydraulic resistance. The theory predicts geometry dependence for plates and cylinders. An initial period of slow contraction is explained through gradual development of pore-liquid pressure as the viscoelastic network compresses the liquid. The companion experimental paper is cited as supporting the theory.

**What it does not establish:** It does not identify the molecular chemistry responsible for network contraction and is not shown, at abstract level, to use industrial sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitated silica. Its constitutive assumptions and parameter range need full-text verification.

**Role and grade:** Important mechanistic framework for coupling permeability, viscoelasticity, sample geometry and syneresis kinetics; class B, MODEL;HYP.

## PSG-038 — Scherer (1989): Mechanics of syneresis II. Experimental study

**Reference:** G. W. Scherer, “Mechanics of syneresis II. Experimental study,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 108(1) (1989), 28–36. DOI: 10.1016/0022-3093(89)90329-3.

**Verification and access:** Elsevier publisher record and abstract inspected; metadata verified. Full text not accessed.

**Study type and system:** Experimental study comparing measured silica-gel shrinkage curves with the continuum framework developed in Part I.

**What it supports:** The abstract reports reasonable agreement between measured shrinkage curves and calculations using independently measured gel permeability, modulus and viscosity. This connects syneresis kinetics to independently characterised transport and mechanical properties rather than fitting shrinkage alone.

**What it does not establish:** Exact precursor chemistry, gel formulation, sample geometry, dimensions, measurement protocols and uncertainty estimates remain to be checked in the full paper. Do not assume this is industrial precipitated silica prepared by sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation.

**Role and grade:** Experimental mechanistic comparator for the transport–mechanics coupling; class B, OBS;MODEL.

## Synthesis implication

The Scherer Parts I–II framework gives a useful physical basis for interpreting the strong geometry dependence observed in Wilhelm & Kind (2015): drainage length and hydraulic resistance interact with network stiffness. However, the Scherer abstracts do not establish that their material chemistry matches industrial precipitated silica. Use the framework to explain why geometry matters, not to transfer numerical parameters or claim a universal microscopic mechanism.
