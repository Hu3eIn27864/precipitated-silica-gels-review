# Source assessment — Butrón, Axelsson & Gustafson (2009): silica-sol hydraulic conductivity and mechanics

**Record:** PSG-047  
**Citation:** C. Butrón, M. Axelsson and G. Gustafson, “Silica sol for rock grouting: Laboratory testing of strength, fracture behaviour and hydraulic conductivity,” *Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology* 24(6) (2009), 603–607. DOI: [10.1016/j.tust.2009.04.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tust.2009.04.003).  
**Evidence class:** B — useful pure silica-sol hydraulic/mechanical comparator; not direct industrial precipitated-silica syneresis evidence.  
**Access:** Chalmers University publication record and publisher abstract inspected; full numerical tables/curves not extracted.

## Material and measurements

The study concerns a refined commercial colloidal silica sol used as a non-cementitious rock grout, with particle sizes reported as 5–100 nm. The authors report laboratory fall-cone, unconfined compression, triaxial and oedometer testing, with specimens examined at different ages and storage environments.

The Chalmers publication record reports:
- initial strength of a few kPa, increasing with age;
- ductile behaviour during the first days, followed by elastic–plastic behaviour;
- hydraulic conductivity in the range 10^-10 to 10^-11 m/s;
- a thin weaker surface layer after immersion in water, extending only a few millimetres into the specimen;
- strength not appreciably changed by a pH environment around 11.

## Relevance and strict transfer limits

This source is useful because it reports hydraulic conductivity and mechanical response for a pure silica-sol gel rather than only a sand–gel composite or a filter cake. It therefore provides an adjacent-system existence proof that low hydraulic conductivity and age-dependent mechanical behaviour can coexist in silica gels.

It does **not** close the target-system parameter gap:
- the material is a commercial colloidal silica sol, not a sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitated silica gel with the particle/fragment history studied by Wilhelm & Kind;
- the reported hydraulic conductivity is not intrinsic permeability (conversion requires fluid density/viscosity and test conditions);
- the accessible abstract does not provide matched conductivity and constrained-modulus values for the same specimen, age, stress state and storage environment;
- full numerical oedometer/strength curves and uncertainties remain to be extracted from the article.

## Evidence disposition

**Defensible use:** comparator for plausible order-of-magnitude hydraulic conductivity and for the qualitative evolution of strength/ductility with gel age in a colloidal-silica grout.

**Not defensible:** direct calibration of precipitated-silica intrinsic permeability, constrained modulus, syneresis time constant or pH-dependent constitutive law.

## Follow-up

If full text is obtained, extract the specimen formulation, solids fraction, particle-size distribution, gel time, age, temperature, storage medium, hydraulic-conductivity method and stress history. Determine whether permeability/conductivity and mechanical response can be paired by specimen state. Convert conductivity to intrinsic permeability only with documented pore-fluid density and viscosity. Keep this source as comparator-only in the evidence matrix.
