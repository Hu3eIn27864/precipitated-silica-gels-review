# Source assessments — batch 12

## PSG-034 — Lucas et al. (2011): silica-sand consolidation by alkaline sodium silicate

**Reference:** S. Lucas, M. T. Tognonvi, J.-L. Gelet, J. Soro and S. Rossignol, “Interactions between silica sand and sodium silicate solution during consolidation process,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 357(4) (2011), 1310–1318. DOI: 10.1016/j.jnoncrysol.2010.12.016.

**Bibliographic verification:** Publisher/ScienceDirect record confirms title, authors, journal, volume, issue, pages, publication date and DOI. The publisher abstract and extensive article preview were inspected; the original full PDF was not independently accessed.

**Study type and material system:** Primary experimental study of natural quartz sand consolidated with commercial alkaline sodium silicate solution (SiO2/Na2O molar ratio 3.47). Three sand distributions are reported, with D50 values of 250, 340 and 560 µm. The silicate solution is diluted to different levels. Consolidation is examined during drying, including 70 and 150 °C conditions.

**Methods and direct measurements:** The publisher preview describes elemental/chemical analysis (ICP), thermal analysis of fresh mixtures, SEM, ATR-FTIR and uniaxial compression testing. The study varies sand size distribution, silicate-solution dilution and drying temperature. The abstract reports free-water release during consolidation and changes in mechanical cohesion with grain size, dilution and drying temperature.

**What the study supports:** In this quartz-sand/waterglass composite, smaller grains and less-diluted silicate solution improve cohesion. Drying temperature changes the resulting interaction. The authors propose a glass-like adhesive silicate layer at 70 °C and a dissolution–precipitation contribution at 150 °C, supported by their combined chemical, thermal, microstructural and mechanical observations.

**What it does not establish:** It does not measure syneresis of an isolated silica gel, nor does it study the post-gel consolidation of amorphous precipitated-silica aggregates formed by sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation. The proposed mechanisms are interpretations, not direct observation of every elementary reaction step. It cannot be used as direct evidence that dissolution–precipitation drives syneresis in industrial precipitated silica.

**Methodological limitations and transferability:** The system contains crystalline quartz grains and alkaline waterglass and is consolidated by drying at elevated temperatures. This differs chemically and physically from a water-rich, amorphous precipitated-silica gel undergoing spontaneous or pressure-driven syneresis. Original full-text methods and uncertainty estimates remain to be checked before quantitative extraction. The paper belongs to the Tognonvi/Rossignol research family; compare with Kouassi et al. (2011) and related pH/interfacial studies before counting independent datasets.

**Intended role:** Mechanistic comparator in a distinct subsection on waterglass-mediated granular consolidation and drying. Keep separate from the central wet-gel syneresis evidence.

**Evidence tags and provisional class:** OBS;HYP, class B.

**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022309310009853

## Research-family note

Lucas et al. (2011) and Kouassi et al. (2011) share Tognonvi/Rossignol authorship and concern alkaline waterglass interactions with silica-based solids. They examine different composite formulations and endpoints, but their independence as experimental programmes has not been established. Treat them as related mechanistic outputs pending comparison of methods, dates, sample formulations and figure-level data.
