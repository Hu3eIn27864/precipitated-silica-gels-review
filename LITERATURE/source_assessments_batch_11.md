## PSG-033 — Kouassi et al. (2011), consolidation of silica-based aggregates with sodium-silicate gel

**Reference:** S. S. Kouassi, M. T. Tognonvi, J. Soro and S. Rossignol, “Consolidation mechanism of materials obtained from sodium silicate solution and silica-based aggregates,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 357(15) (2011), 3013–3021. DOI: 10.1016/j.jnoncrysol.2011.04.006.

**Bibliographic verification:** ScienceDirect publisher record and INIST/Pascal record agree on title, author list, journal, volume, issue, pages and DOI. Publisher abstract and extended indexed article text inspected; original publisher PDF not inspected.

**System / study type:** Primary experimental study of granular consolidation using an alkaline sodium-silicate gel as a binder. Concentrated waterglass ([Si] = 7.01 mol/L, Si/Na = 1.71, pH 11.56) is acidified with 0.5 mol/L HCl. Irreversible gels are formed in the basic pH range (reported pH 10–11) and mixed with crystalline silica powder (D50 = 10 µm) and sands with mean sizes of 90 and 560 µm. The gelation time used for casting is 55 min.

**Directly reported in publisher abstract/preview:** The gel–silica–sand composition domain depends on sand size. FTIR monitoring shows a broad Si–O–Si band shifting to lower wavenumber up to 21 days and then to higher wavenumber; the authors interpret this as evidence consistent with dissolution/precipitation reactions. Weight-loss and compressive-strength measurements are used during consolidation. Increasing fine-silica content increases weight loss and compressive strength. The authors propose dissolution of fine silica particles and precipitation at sand grain boundaries as a consolidation mechanism. The article introduction explicitly notes that irreversible alkaline sodium-silicate gels contract by syneresis and remain stable in water.

**What this supports:** Alkaline waterglass gel maturation and silica-particle interactions can contribute to consolidation of a granular skeleton; FTIR evolution and mechanical response are consistent with a dissolution/precipitation contribution. It provides a useful bridge between gel chemistry and interparticle/grain-boundary bonding.

**What it does not establish:** It does not directly quantify the syneresis of an isolated precipitated-silica gel fragment. Weight loss of a composite gel–silica–sand specimen cannot be assigned uniquely to syneresis; it may include other water-loss or reaction-related contributions. It is not a sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid industrial precipitated silica process and does not report wet-gel aggregate porosity or BET endpoint data in the accessible abstract.

**Methodological limitations / transferability:** Original full text and detailed weight-loss protocol, curing humidity, sample mass balance, FTIR fitting, replicate counts and compressive-test conditions remain to be checked. The silica aggregates are crystalline quartz-based powders/sands, whereas precipitated silica primary particles are amorphous and nanoscale. The dissolution/precipitation mechanism is a proposed interpretation, not a directly imaged molecular pathway.

**Research-family note:** This work shares authors and research context with Tognonvi/Rossignol studies of alkaline sodium-silicate gels and consolidation. It should not be treated as an independent replication of those studies without comparing gel recipes and datasets.

**Review role:** Mechanistic comparator for alkaline gel-mediated granular consolidation and possible dissolution/precipitation at silica interfaces. **Provisional class B; OBS+HYP.**

## Historical source H-001 — Holmes, Kaufmann & Nicholas (1919)

**Reference:** H. N. Holmes, W. E. Kaufmann and H. O. Nicholas, “The Vibration and Syneresis of Silicic Acid Gels,” *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 41(9) (1919), 1329–1336. DOI: 10.1021/ja02230a002.

**Bibliographic verification:** ACS publisher record confirms title, authors, journal, volume, issue, date, pages and DOI. Publisher page indicates that the article is available only through purchase; only the first-page preview and bibliographic record were accessible.

**System / study type:** Historical experimental paper on vibration and syneresis of silicic-acid gels. The exact gel preparation, composition, vibration protocol, syneresis metric and numerical findings have not been verified from the full text.

**What it can currently support:** Only the historical fact that vibration and syneresis of silicic-acid gels were studied experimentally by 1919.

**What it cannot currently support:** Any claim about whether vibration accelerates or suppresses syneresis, the physical mechanism, gel composition, magnitude or kinetics. The title is not evidence of a directional effect.

**Review role:** Historical retrieval target only. **Status V (verification pending); no substantive evidence grade assigned.** Do not cite for mechanistic claims until the original paper is obtained and read.

## Synthesis note

The 1919 and 1930 papers are historically relevant but currently cannot be used as evidence beyond bibliographic context. Kouassi et al. (2011) add a separate alkaline granular-consolidation comparator, not a new direct measurement of syneresis in industrial precipitated silica. Keep these categories distinct in the review.
