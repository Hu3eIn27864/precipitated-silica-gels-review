# Research-family overlap audit — Schlomach / Quarch

**Assessment date:** 11 November 2026  
**Sources:** Jan Schlomach (2006) dissertation; Kerstin Quarch (2010) dissertation; Schlomach & Kind (2004), *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science*.

## 1. Direct evidence of overlap

Quarch’s dissertation states in §4.2.1 (“Standardversuch”) that the measurements shown for the standard semi-batch process are **repeat experiments of the experiments conducted in the preceding work by Schlomach (2006)** and that the results are reproducible. This is explicit attribution by the later experimental programme, not an inference based solely on common authors or institution.

The same section presents the time-dependent particle-size development before gelation and post-gel fragment-size distributions. Quarch also explicitly refers back to Schlomach (2006) for primary-particle formation kinetics and for process variations already studied.

Schlomach’s dissertation bibliography cites the 2004 paper “Investigations on the semi-batch precipitation of silica” by Schlomach and Kind. Thus the dissertation and article are linked bibliographically within the same research line. This does not, by itself, prove every figure in the 2004 paper is identical to a dissertation figure; that exact article-to-thesis figure concordance remains open.

## 2. What is duplicated and what is additional?

| Dataset / topic | Schlomach (2006) | Quarch (2010) | Evidence-counting decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard semi-batch silica precipitation | Original experimental programme | Explicitly described as repeat experiments; reproducible | Same lineage; not independent replication |
| Primary-particle formation and pre-gel kinetics | Detailed precursor study | Refers back to Schlomach for primary-particle formation kinetics; adds further characterisation | Treat direct repeat runs as repeatability evidence, not independent confirmation |
| Post-gel particle/aggregate size and fractal restructuring | Core earlier programme | Repeats standard process and adds extended time-resolved characterisation | Map figures and conditions before counting separately |
| Parameter variation in semi-batch operation | Temperature, salt, feed rate, stirrer speed among studied variables | Explicitly notes prior parameter variations; adds room-temperature and half-feed-rate tests and other studies | Some conditions are inherited; some are additional experiments |
| Quarch rheology of unstirred gels | Not the central Schlomach programme | Dedicated Y-mixer/rheology experiments over composition and pH | Additional experimental evidence |
| Quarch batch fragmentation, stirred tank/Taylor–Couette and ultrasound | Not the same standard semi-batch repeat dataset | Additional experimental programme | Distinct experiments within same research family |
| Schlomach & Kind (2004) journal paper | Cited in dissertation | Cited as prior work | Closely related peer-reviewed output; exact figure-level overlap with thesis still to be checked |

## 3. Important scientific distinction

The Quarch dissertation should not be treated as a wholesale duplicate of Schlomach (2006). It contains an explicitly repeated standard semi-batch experiment, but also adds substantial new work: unstirred-gel rheology, batch fragmentation, reactor comparisons, ultrasound resistance and early ageing observations.

Conversely, the fact that Quarch repeated the standard semi-batch process and found reproducible results is useful evidence of within-programme repeatability. It is not external replication by an independent group. The review should say so precisely.

## 4. Implications for the literature synthesis

1. **Use Schlomach (2006) for detailed early process-history and semi-batch evidence**, and Schlomach & Kind (2004) as the peer-reviewed article output where its reported claims match the relevant dataset.
2. **Use Quarch (2010) for genuinely additional gel-rheology and fragmentation evidence**, while flagging the standard semi-batch repeat as same-lineage repeatability.
3. **Do not count the standard semi-batch experiments in Schlomach and Quarch as two independent studies.**
4. **Do not overstate the article/dissertation overlap** until figures and experimental condition tables in Schlomach & Kind (2004) are compared directly with the thesis.
5. **Preserve the methodological limitations**: Quarch explicitly warns about the assumptions in DLS-derived size values and the limits imposed by increasing suspension viscosity.

## 5. Review-ready formulation

“Several foundational results on semi-batch precipitation and post-gel restructuring originate from a connected KIT research programme. Quarch explicitly identifies the standard semi-batch measurements in the 2010 dissertation as reproducible repeats of Schlomach’s 2006 experiments. The later work nevertheless extends this foundation through additional rheological, batch-fragmentation and reactor studies. Accordingly, the repeated standard-process data should be treated as within-programme repeatability, whereas the additional experimental modules may provide distinct evidence.”

**Confidence:** high that Quarch’s standard semi-batch dataset repeats Schlomach (2006), based on explicit statement in Quarch §4.2.1; moderate for exact overlap between Schlomach & Kind (2004) and the 2006 dissertation pending figure-level concordance.
