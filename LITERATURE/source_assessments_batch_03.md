## PSG-019 — Schlomach (2006), doctoral dissertation

**Verified reference:** Jan Schlomach, *Feststoffbildung bei technischen Fällprozessen: Untersuchungen zur industriellen Fällung von Siliziumdioxid und Calciumcarbonat*. Dissertation, Universität Karlsruhe (TH), 2006. Universitätsverlag Karlsruhe, IX + 275 pp. ISBN 3-86644-024-3. DOI: 10.5445/KSP/1000004369.

**Verification:** Full institutional PDF inspected. KIT library catalogue independently confirms title, author, publisher, year, 275 pages and dissertation status. The thesis states that the work was carried out July 2000–June 2005 and defended on 20 March 2006.

**System / study type:** Original experimental and modelling programme on industrially relevant silica precipitation from sodium silicate and sulfuric acid, with calcium carbonate as a second, distinct model system. Only the silica part is relevant to this review.

### Directly reported silica evidence

The thesis separates the precipitation pathway into primary-particle formation, aggregation to gelation, and post-gel evolution. It experimentally examines pre-gel particle-formation kinetics, gel-point behaviour, post-gel particle-size evolution and solid properties. The investigated process variables include mechanical energy input, sodium sulfate concentration, sodium-silicate feed rate and temperature.

After the gel point, the reported Sauter diameter decreases as mechanical energy input increases, consistent with breakup of gel clusters under stronger shear. The post-gel particle-size distributions can develop multiple modes; in one high-salt condition, a later third peak is discussed, although the thesis itself notes that an early indication of that peak may be an artefact. The fractal dimension rises rapidly after the gel point from approximately 1.7–1.85 toward a condition-dependent plateau, generally in the approximate 2.1–2.4 range for the illustrated experiments. It is not a single invariant value: salt, feed rate, energy input and temperature alter the trajectory.

The thesis also tests structural-reorganisation models. One proposed mechanism involving deposition/uptake of newly formed primary particles does not reproduce the high experimental fractal dimensions; the author therefore considers additional restructuring/densification mechanisms. These are model interpretations, not direct imaging of the microscopic rearrangement process.

### What this supports

- The post-gel state continues to evolve; gelation is not the end of particle/aggregate restructuring.
- Mechanical energy input and chemical/process conditions jointly affect post-gel particle size and inferred aggregate structure.
- A single “fractal dimension of precipitated silica” is not defensible without specifying time after gel point and process conditions.
- The thesis gives a fuller methodological and parameter context for the semi-batch precipitation study than the short 2004 journal article abstract alone.

### What it does not establish

- That the observed post-gel trends are universal across all precipitated-silica formulations or reactor scales.
- That a fitted fractal dimension uniquely identifies a particular microscopic restructuring mechanism.
- That the modelled primary-particle deposition mechanism is sufficient: the thesis itself shows that this model does not reproduce the high experimental values.
- Independent replication of the 2004 Schlomach & Kind paper or of later Quarch semi-batch work.

### Methodological limitations / transferability

The work combines optical/scattering-based particle characterisation, rheology, process measurements and structural modelling. Fractal dimensions are inferred from measurement/model relationships and should not be treated as direct microscopy-derived structural invariants. Reactor-specific hydrodynamics and the coupling of mixing, feed addition and reaction kinetics constrain scale-up. Calcium carbonate results are not evidence for silica and must be kept outside the silica-specific synthesis.

**Overlap assessment:** Quarch’s 2010 dissertation explicitly states that its standard semi-batch experiments repeat experiments from Schlomach (2006). Schlomach & Kind (2004), *JCIS*, is a closely related peer-reviewed output from this programme. These sources are not three independent replications. Use Schlomach (2006) as the detailed long-form methods/results source, Schlomach & Kind (2004) as the peer-reviewed synthesis of the semi-batch pathway, and Quarch (2010) only for genuinely additional experiments or later extensions. Exact figure-by-figure overlap still needs mapping.

**Review role:** Core evidence for precipitation, aggregation, post-gel restructuring and process-variable dependence. **Class A; OBS+CALC, with model claims separately tagged MODEL.**

## PSG-008 — Wilhelm (2017), doctoral dissertation

**Verified reference:** Sebastian Maximilian Wilhelm, *Über die Synärese kolloidaler Strukturen am Beispiel von Siliciumdioxid*. Dissertation, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, 2017. KIT Scientific Publishing, 218 pp. ISBN 978-3-7315-0709-3. DOI: 10.5445/KSP/1000073100. Defence: 6 July 2017.

**Verification:** Full institutional PDF inspected. Title page, imprint, DOI, ISBN and defence date are explicit in the PDF. The thesis research was carried out November 2011–October 2016.

**System / study type:** Original experimental and modelling programme on acid- and base-catalysed precipitated silica gels. It covers natural syneresis, mechanically enforced syneresis using pressure and centrifugal fields, sample-size effects, pore-liquid monomer concentration, and filtration-based models with internal driving forces.

### Directly reported

The thesis proposes interpreting syneresis broadly as compaction of a porous solid network accompanied by expulsion of pore liquid, and therefore as a discontinuous filtration process with a compressible cake. It develops two experimental approaches to enforced syneresis: a pressure-cell arrangement and centrifugation. It examines pH, temperature, initial sample size and external force.

The abstract reports analogous qualitative parameter dependencies for natural and enforced syneresis: base-catalysed gels show greater maximum compaction than acid-catalysed gels at comparable external force, and increasing temperature accelerates compaction. However, the thesis explicitly reports that prediction of natural syneresis from enforced syneresis can show both good agreement and substantial deviations. The empirical extrapolation is highly sensitive to measurements at low external forces.

The thesis directly reproduces natural-syneresis curves from Wilhelm & Kind (2015) and cites both Wilhelm & Kind (2014) and (2015) in its bibliography. For example, the natural-syneresis curves for 12 mm, approximately 3 mL cylinders are explicitly captioned as “[Wilhelm u. Kind, 2015]”. This is decisive evidence that at least those figures are not new independent datasets in the dissertation.

The thesis adds substantial detail beyond the journal articles: a second enforced-syneresis configuration based on centrifugal force, a more extensive treatment of pore-liquid monomer concentration and membrane retention, and expanded physically based modelling. It compares initial sample volumes and characteristic drainage lengths; the pressure cell includes 15 and 20 mL samples, while centrifugation uses 1 and 0.33 mL samples. The thesis notes that reducing sample dimensions can reduce syneresis times from hundreds of hours to only a few hours in the tested geometries.

The pore-liquid analysis is important for mechanism: acid-catalysed gels show a rapid decrease in dissolved monomer concentration to near/below the cited solubility limit, whereas base-catalysed gels show a different temporal profile. This makes ongoing silica dissolution/condensation a relevant consideration when interpreting measured volume change, but the measured pore-liquid concentration alone does not uniquely identify the network-scale driving force.

### What this supports

- Syneresis is coupled compaction and pore-liquid transport, not simply a synonym for drying shrinkage.
- pH, temperature and initial sample geometry affect both magnitude and kinetics.
- Enforced syneresis is a useful accelerated characterisation method, but the extrapolation to natural syneresis is sensitive to low-force data and model assumptions.
- Pore-liquid chemistry should be considered when interpreting time-dependent shrinkage and when deciding whether the process is purely mechanical filtration.

### What it does not establish

- That natural and forced syneresis are microscopically identical.
- That the empirical or physically extended models predict natural syneresis reliably for all formulations and sample geometries.
- That the 2014/2015 journal results and the dissertation’s reproduced plots are independent observations.
- That a single filtration model captures all internal forces or chemistry; several internal driving-force terms are fitted because they are not independently known.

### Methodological limitations / transferability

The physical model includes internal driving forces whose values are obtained by fitting to enforced-syneresis data; this is calibration, not an independent measurement of those forces. Predictions of natural syneresis inherit the uncertainty of the enforced-data extrapolation, especially near zero applied force. The sample-size effect is confounded with drainage length, surface-to-volume ratio and geometry; it should not be reduced to a simple particle-size law. Pressure-cell and centrifuge boundary conditions differ from freely syneresing fragments in a stirred industrial reactor. The experimental gels are controlled model formulations and do not span the full industrial composition/process space.

**Overlap assessment:** Wilhelm & Kind (2014) and (2015) are explicitly cited as published outputs in the dissertation; at least the 2015 natural-syneresis curves are reproduced in the thesis. Therefore, the thesis, 2014 article and 2015 article constitute one research family. The thesis is the richest source for methods, additional centrifugation experiments, pore-liquid analysis and modelling; the journal articles remain concise peer-reviewed reports. Do not count duplicated 2015 curves as fresh replication. A detailed figure/table concordance between 2014/2015 articles and thesis remains a useful final audit.

**Review role:** Core evidence for natural/enforced syneresis, pH/temperature/sample-size effects, pore-liquid chemistry and model uncertainty. **Class A; OBS+CALC+MODEL, with fitted internal driving forces explicitly identified as model parameters.**

## Consequences for the review

1. **The evidence base is smaller than the raw publication count suggests.** Multiple papers and dissertations are related outputs from a small number of coordinated KIT research programmes.
2. **Dissertations remain valuable primary-source records.** They provide experimental conditions, rawer context, limitations and additional methods, but should not inflate the number of independent studies.
3. **The review should cite the most informative source for each claim.** Use the journal article where it reports the peer-reviewed result; use the dissertation to substantiate methodological detail or additional experiments not present in the article.
4. **The main evidence gap is external replication.** Much of the direct industrial precipitated-silica evidence comes from a tightly connected research lineage. This is a limitation to state openly, not a reason to discard the work.
5. **The review’s novelty should be critical integration, not a claim that the process sequence has never been described.** The contribution is to connect process history, gel mechanics, fragmentation, consolidation and syneresis while distinguishing direct measurements from fitted mechanisms and duplicated datasets.
