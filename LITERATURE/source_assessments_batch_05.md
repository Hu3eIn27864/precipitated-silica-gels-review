# Individual source assessment — batch 05

**Assessment date:** 11 November 2026  
**Purpose:** capture independent evidence for structural ageing of commercial precipitated silica, while keeping powder ageing separate from wet-gel consolidation and syneresis.

## PSG-022 — Chal et al. (2019), “Nanostructured silica used in super-insulation materials (SIM), hygrothermal ageing followed by sorption characterizations”

**Reference:** B. Chal, B. Yrieix, L. Roiban, K. Masenelli-Varlot, J.-M. Chenal and G. Foray, “Nanostructured silica used in super-insulation materials (SIM), hygrothermal ageing followed by sorption characterizations”, *Energy and Buildings* 183 (2019), 626–638. DOI: 10.1016/j.enbuild.2018.11.044.

**Verification status:** Publisher bibliographic record and abstract inspected; full article not inspected. The publisher record confirms publication on 15 January 2019, volume 183, pages 626–638, and DOI.

**System / methods:** Nine commercial products grouped into precipitated silica, fumed silica, hydrophobized silica and silica aerogels. Powder and aerogel samples were exposed in climatic chambers at reference conditions (23 °C/50% RH) and accelerated conditions of 50 or 70 °C combined with 70 or 90% relative humidity. Nitrogen and water sorption were used to assess specific surface area, pore-size distribution and hydrophilicity.

**What the abstract and publisher preview report:** Ageing commonly reduces specific surface area and shifts the pore-size distribution through loss of the smallest mesopores. The magnitude and pattern depend on silica type and surface chemistry. Hydrophilic precipitated silica is more sensitive than fumed silica in the tested materials. The authors propose a mechanism involving mass transfer through dissolution/precipitation, requiring adsorbed water and hydrophilic surface groups. For precipitated and fumed powders, temperature is especially important in initiating changes over the first 24 days, while relative humidity influences the intensity over the longer 96-day exposure.

**What this supports:** Commercial precipitated silica is not structurally static after manufacture and drying. Water availability, temperature, surface silanol chemistry and exposure time can alter accessible surface area and mesopore populations. This gives an independent, industrially relevant bridge between silica surface chemistry and slower structural ageing.

**What it does not establish:** The study does not investigate wet precipitated-silica gel fragments during the hours immediately after gelation, does not measure natural syneresis, and does not directly quantify consolidation pressure, permeability or fragment mechanics. Its results cannot be used as direct proof of the wet-gel consolidation mechanism.

**Limitations / transferability:** The work compares several commercial product classes and accelerated climatic conditions. Product-specific formulation, drying history and surface treatment may affect response. Full text and product table must be inspected before extracting precipitated-silica-specific quantitative data or comparing magnitudes across products. The proposed dissolution/precipitation mechanism is an interpretation supported by sorption and surface-chemistry trends, not direct real-time imaging of mass transfer.

**Review role:** Independent evidence for post-manufacture ageing of commercial precipitated silica powders and for the sensitivity of structure to humidity, temperature and surface chemistry. Use in a clearly delimited section on post-drying ageing / final-product stability, not as a substitute for wet-gel syneresis evidence. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC, with mechanism tagged HYP.**

## Boundary condition for synthesis

The manuscript should distinguish at least three temporally and physically different phenomena:

1. **Wet-gel consolidation / syneresis:** compaction of a liquid-filled porous network and expulsion of pore liquid after gelation.
2. **Aqueous-gel ageing:** chemical and structural evolution within water-glass-derived or precipitated aqueous gels, potentially involving dissolution, transport and recondensation.
3. **Post-drying powder ageing:** changes in commercial precipitated silica powders under humid/thermal exposure, affecting surface area, mesoporosity and surface chemistry.

These phenomena may share dissolution/precipitation or siloxane-condensation motifs, but they differ in initial state, transport pathways, boundary conditions and observables. The review must not collapse them into a single undifferentiated “ageing” process.
