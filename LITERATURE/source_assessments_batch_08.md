## PSG-030 — Quang et al. (2012), gelation history and pilot-scale powder properties

**Reference:** D. V. Quang, J.-K. Kim, J.-K. Park, S.-H. Park, G. Elineema, P. B. Sarawade and H. T. Kim, “Effect of the gelation on the properties of precipitated silica powder produced by acidizing sodium silicate solution at the pilot scale”, *Chemical Engineering Journal* 209 (2012), 531–536. DOI: 10.1016/j.cej.2012.08.028.

**Verification level:** ScienceDirect publisher record and expanded abstract/highlights/conclusion preview inspected. Full article PDF and complete methods/figures/tables have **not** been inspected.

**System / process:** Pilot-scale precipitated silica produced by acidifying sodium silicate in the presence of sodium chloride. Sulfuric acid is added in two stages. The first stage ends before the gelation point; the second ends at pH 5. The interval Δt between the end of the first acidification stage and the gelation point is the key process variable. The final powder is characterised by nitrogen adsorption–desorption, FE-SEM and FE-STEM.

**Directly reported results in the publisher preview:**
- BET specific surface area and pore volume decrease as Δt increases.
- BET specific surface area and pore volume also decrease with increasing reaction temperature.
- Pore size is reported as not clearly affected / not showing a clear trend.
- The primary-particle size is reported to be larger at higher reaction temperature.
- The authors use a percolation model to interpret flocculation/bridging and the resulting packing arrangement.

**What this supports:** This is unusually useful process-to-product evidence at pilot scale. It supports the bounded claim that the timing of gelation relative to staged acid addition, as well as reaction temperature, affects final powder surface area and pore volume. It demonstrates that the pre-gel interval matters to final product properties; the authors report that the stage before gelation dominates more than the acid addition after gelation under their tested process.

**What it does not establish:** The study does not directly measure natural syneresis, wet-gel shrinkage, pore-liquid expulsion or time-resolved wet-network densification. The final BET/pore-volume differences cannot be assigned specifically to syneresis. The percolation/packing account is a model-based interpretation, not a direct image of the entire wet-gel evolution.

**Limitations / transferability:** Although pilot-scale, the process includes sodium chloride, staged acidification and subsequent powder work-up. Washing, drying and other post-gel treatments may influence final sorption properties. The available preview does not provide full numerical values, replicate counts, uncertainty, exact temperature range, solids basis or complete operating conditions. Extract these from the full article before quoting numerical trends or treating the model as validated beyond this process.

**Review role:** Strong candidate for the process-history → gelation/flocculation → final powder-property section. It should be kept separate from direct wet-gel syneresis evidence. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC, with MODEL interpretation.** The reported direction of the endpoint trends is now visible, but the absence of full methods/data appraisal limits confidence in quantitative detail.

---

## Synthesis note

Quang et al. provide a bridge between gelation history and final powder properties, not proof that syneresis controls those properties. A careful review can use the work to argue that the pre-gel process window can leave a persistent signature in the final powder. It should then explicitly ask how much of that signature survives or is modified by post-gel ageing, washing and drying.
