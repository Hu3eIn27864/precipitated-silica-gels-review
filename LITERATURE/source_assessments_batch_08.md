## PSG-030 — Quang et al. (2012), gelation and pilot-scale precipitated silica

**Reference:** D. V. Quang, J.-K. Kim, J.-K. Park, S.-H. Park, G. Elineema, P. B. Sarawade and H. T. Kim, “Effect of the gelation on the properties of precipitated silica powder produced by acidizing sodium silicate solution at the pilot scale”, *Chemical Engineering Journal* 209 (2012), 531–536. DOI: 10.1016/j.cej.2012.08.028.

**Bibliographic verification:** Publisher record confirms title, author list, journal, volume, pages, date and DOI. The article is labelled a short communication.

**Source-integrity note:** The ScienceDirect page preview is internally contaminated: after a relevant opening abstract passage describing staged acidification of sodium silicate/NaCl and the pilot-scale effect of gelation, it appends text about MTMS/TMCS-modified aerogels and calcination that does not belong to this study. This initially prompted quarantine. Cross-checking the ResearchGate page for the same publication (including figure caption/context and an excerpt attributed to the full text) confirms the paper's actual subject and key abstract claims. The appended aerogel material is therefore treated as a page/indexing contamination, not as the article's content. The original publisher PDF has still not been independently inspected.

**System / process:** Pilot-scale precipitated silica from sodium silicate acidified with H2SO4 in the presence of NaCl. Acid is added in two stages: the first stage ends before the gel point, and the second ends at pH 5. The interval Δt between the end of the first acidification stage and gelation is recorded as a process variable. Samples are characterised by nitrogen adsorption–desorption, FE-SEM and FE-STEM. The effect of Δt is examined at 40 and 60 °C.

**Results supported by the accessible full-text excerpts / article highlights:**
- BET specific surface area decreases as Δt increases at both 40 and 60 °C.
- At Δt close to zero, batch-to-batch BET values span approximately 200–350 m²/g; this spread narrows as Δt increases.
- The abstract/highlights report that BET surface area and pore volume decrease as Δt and reaction temperature increase.
- Pore size is reported not to be affected by these variables.
- Primary-particle size is reported to be larger at higher reaction temperature.
- The authors interpret flocculation/bridging using a percolation model. SEM/TEM excerpts describe primary particles connected into a network and report a higher pore fraction/unoccupied-bond fraction at lower Δt.

**What this supports:** This is relevant pilot-scale evidence that the timing of gelation relative to staged acid addition can leave a measurable signature in final precipitated-silica powder surface area and pore volume. It supports a process-history → flocculation/network architecture → final powder-property link in the specific tested formulation.

**What it does not establish:** The study does not directly measure natural syneresis, wet-gel shrinkage, pore-liquid expulsion or time-resolved wet-gel densification. Therefore, the final BET/pore-volume trends cannot be attributed specifically to syneresis. The percolation model is an interpretation of the observed particle/network arrangement, not a direct time-resolved measurement of the entire wet-gel pathway.

**Limitations / transferability:** Pilot-scale relevance is valuable, but the process includes NaCl, staged acidification and subsequent work-up. Washing and drying may influence final sorption properties. The currently accessible excerpts do not provide complete numerical datasets, uncertainty, replicate counts, all operating conditions or full model details. The original publisher PDF should be obtained before quantitative pooling or detailed methodological appraisal.

**Review role:** Important candidate source for process-history → gelation/flocculation → final powder properties. Keep separate from direct wet-gel syneresis evidence. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC, with MODEL interpretation.** The directional trends are supported by article highlights and excerpts, but the incomplete full-text appraisal limits confidence in numerical detail.

---

## Synthesis note

The study can be cited cautiously for directional pilot-scale trends, with the exact material route and post-gel work-up stated. It must not be used as proof that syneresis controls final BET area. The source's abstract/page contamination should be noted in the internal audit, and the original PDF should be retrieved before manuscript submission.
