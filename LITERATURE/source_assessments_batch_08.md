## PSG-030 — Quang et al. (2012), gelation history and pilot-scale powder properties

**Reference:** D. V. Quang, J.-K. Kim, J.-K. Park, S.-H. Park, G. Elineema, P. B. Sarawade and H. T. Kim, “Effect of the gelation on the properties of precipitated silica powder produced by acidizing sodium silicate solution at the pilot scale”, *Chemical Engineering Journal* 209 (2012), 531–536. DOI: 10.1016/j.cej.2012.08.028.

**Verification:** Publisher record and abstract inspected. Author list, journal, volume, pages and DOI are available from the publisher record. Full text has not yet been inspected.

**System / process:** Pilot-scale precipitated silica produced by acidifying sodium silicate in the presence of sodium chloride. The abstract describes sulfuric acid addition in two stages: the first stage ends before the gelation point; the second ends when the pH reaches 5. The stated outcomes include BET specific surface area and pore volume of the final powder.

**Directly established from the abstract:** The study explicitly investigates the effect of gelation on BET surface area and pore volume at pilot scale. The staged acidification scheme makes gelation timing a controlled process variable.

**Supports:** This is a potentially important bridge between process history/gelation and measurable properties of the dried precipitated-silica product. It broadens the evidence base beyond laboratory-scale gel-fragment and syneresis studies.

**Does not yet establish:** Without the full text, we cannot state the direction or magnitude of BET/pore-volume changes, exact operating conditions, replicate numbers, uncertainty, or whether the gel point was determined rheologically or by another operational criterion. It does not, on the abstract alone, establish that natural wet-gel syneresis caused the final powder-property differences.

**Limitations / transferability:** Pilot-scale relevance is valuable, but process configuration, solids concentration, electrolyte content, washing and drying history may all influence final sorption properties. These variables must be extracted before connecting the results to wet-gel network consolidation. BET and pore volume are endpoint measurements and do not directly resolve the wet network structure.

**Review role:** Candidate primary source for the process-history → gelation → final powder-property link. Keep separate from direct wet-gel syneresis evidence. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC.**

## Synthesis note

The source may strengthen the final-properties section, but it should not yet be used to claim a mechanistic link between syneresis and BET area. The defensible statement at this stage is narrower: a pilot-scale study explicitly varied gelation history and measured final powder BET area and pore volume. Full-text extraction is required before making a directional or quantitative claim.
