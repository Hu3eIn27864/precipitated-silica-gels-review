# Research-family overlap audit — Frolov–Shabanova–Silos silica hydrosol studies

## Purpose

Map the older Russian-language research line on colloidal silica hydrosol stability, polycondensation, electrolyte coagulation and sol–gel transition. Prevent publication counts from being mistaken for independent replications, and separate hydrosol comparators from industrial sodium-silicate/acid precipitation.

## What can and cannot be inferred about overlap

- Shared authorship across 1983–1996 establishes a coherent research programme, not necessarily repeated use of the same batches.
- The 1984 polycondensation/coagulation paper and 1996 electrolyte-induced sol–gel paper address related but distinct questions and report different methods in their abstracts. Do not count them as independent replications of the same quantitative threshold.
- The 1987 *Langmuir* paper overlaps conceptually with the 1983/1996 stability and gelation work, but figure/table-level comparison is impossible while the full text remains unavailable.
- The 1993 mineral-acid paper addresses acid identity. Its preformed colloidal hydrosol is not equivalent to simultaneous sodium-silicate/strong-acid precipitation.
- No raw-data or figure-level overlap has been established. Represent the papers as one research family with complementary studies, not independent confirmation.

## Material-system boundary

These papers concern colloidal silica hydrosols and silicic-acid polymerization/coagulation. The exact silica source and preparation route vary or remain unverified. They are mechanistic comparators for interfacial charge, electrolyte effects, particle growth and gelation.

They do **not** directly establish the behaviour of freshly precipitated industrial silica formed in a stirred/semi-batch sodium-silicate/strong-acid process, nor the subsequent natural syneresis, pore-liquid drainage or consolidation of those industrial gels.

## Bibliographic cautions

- Ponomareva et al. (1984): pages 118–120 in the English translation; Russian-language bibliographies give 140–142 in the original. Retain both pending inspection of both editions.
- Shabanova & Silos (1996): pages 256–261 in the English translation and 266–271 in the Russian original.
- Shabanova et al. (1984): volume 46(5), pages 986–993. One later secondary list misprints volume 51; use volume 46, supported by an indexed record and an open article's reference list.
- Kontorovich et al. (1988) modulus paper is traceable through a university repository bibliography, but the original article and abstract remain unavailable.

## Recommended use in the review

Use the hydrosol family in a bounded subsection on pH/electrolyte-dependent colloidal stability, polycondensation and aggregation-to-gel transition. Clearly label it as colloidal-hydrosol evidence. Do not use it as quantitative evidence for industrial precipitated-silica syneresis until full texts and material formulations are verified.
