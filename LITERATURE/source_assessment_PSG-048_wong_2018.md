# Source assessment PSG-048 — Wong et al. (2018): colloidal-silica gel

## Bibliographic record

Wong, C.; Pedrotti, M.; El Mountassir, G.; Lunn, R. J. (2018). “A study on the mechanical interaction between soil and colloidal silica gel for ground improvement.” *Engineering Geology* 243, 84–100. DOI: [10.1016/j.enggeo.2018.06.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enggeo.2018.06.011).

Associated open dataset: Wong et al. (2019), “Data for: A study on the mechanical interaction between soil and colloidal silica gel for ground improvement”, University of Strathclyde, DOI [10.15129/370eb0a3-8d84-406c-b47a-f0aa87ce291c](https://doi.org/10.15129/370eb0a3-8d84-406c-b47a-f0aa87ce291c). The repository record identifies an XLSX dataset (17 MB) under CC BY 4.0.

## Material and preparation

The pure-gel comparator is commercial Meyco MP320 colloidal silica: 40 wt% silica, nominal particle size 15 nm, density 1300 kg m^-3. Colloidal silica and electrolyte were mixed 5:1 by volume. Unless otherwise specified, a 1.7 M NaCl electrolyte stock was used, giving 0.28 M final electrolyte concentration and an approximately one-hour gel time. Samples gelled at 20 °C and 90% RH, then were cured under demineralised water at 20 °C. Oedometer samples were prepared in 75 mm diameter, 20 mm thick rings. The curing-age series included 1, 2, 4 and 8 weeks.

This is an electrolyte-triggered colloidal-silica sol–gel system. It is not the sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation route used for the target industrial precipitated-silica gels. It must remain a comparator class, not be pooled with target-system data.

## Transport and mechanical evidence

The paper reports one-dimensional compression/void-ratio curves for pure colloidal-silica gel, including curing-age and preparation-condition comparisons. Hydraulic conductivity was calculated from the oedometer consolidation response using the Taylor t90 method at each loading step. The reported range is approximately 10^-9 to 10^-12 m s^-1; conductivity generally decreases as effective vertical stress increases. The authors note high variability at early loading steps, partly because t90 is difficult to estimate for these long-duration tests.

The curves and associated dataset create a useful opportunity to reconstruct stress-dependent compressibility and compare it with the reported hydraulic-conductivity series. A constrained modulus could potentially be estimated from the slope of the void-ratio/compression relation after converting to the appropriate strain measure and defining the stress interval. Such a modulus would be state- and stress-dependent; it must not be treated as a single material constant without justification.

## What can and cannot be inferred

- **Supported:** this particular colloidal-silica gel exhibits stress- and curing-history-dependent compression and a reported hydraulic-conductivity range of about 10^-9–10^-12 m/s.
- **Potentially extractable:** paired, state-indexed compression and conductivity records from the openly deposited workbook, subject to inspection of workbook sheets, specimen identifiers, stress steps, replicates and units.
- **Not yet established:** a directly measured intrinsic permeability paired with a constrained modulus for an identical specimen state. Hydraulic conductivity is not intrinsic permeability; conversion requires fluid density, gravity and viscosity at the actual test conditions.
- **Not transferable without validation:** numerical transport or stiffness parameters for sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitated silica, natural syneresis, or the membrane/cake geometries in Wilhelm & Kind.
- **Not a direct syneresis dataset:** the experiments are oedometer consolidation tests under applied vertical stress, not unforced free syneresis.

## Relevance and next action

Grade B comparator evidence. This is more useful than a cake-only filtration source because pure gel compression and conductivity are both addressed, and an openly licensed dataset is available. The next concrete task is to inspect the XLSX workbook, identify the pure-CS sheets and specimen-state mapping, and digitise/reconstruct paired conductivity–compressibility observations with uncertainty and provenance. Do not claim matched M–kappa pairs until the workbook structure and exact stress-state alignment have been checked.
