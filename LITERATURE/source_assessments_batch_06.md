# Individual source assessments — batch 06

**Assessment date:** 11 November 2026  
**Purpose:** recover early independent experimental literature on sodium-silicate-derived silica gelation, ageing and surface properties. These sources predate the modern precipitated-silica process studies and provide valuable historical context, but their material preparation, washing and drying protocols must be kept explicit.

---

## PSG-023 — Sing & Madeley (1953), pH and surface properties

**Reference:** K. S. W. Sing and J. D. Madeley, “The surface properties of silica gels. I. Importance of pH in the preparation from sodium silicate and sulphuric acid”, *Journal of Applied Chemistry* 3(12) (1953), 549–556. DOI: 10.1002/jctb.5010031205.

**Verification:** Wiley publisher metadata and DOI verified; abstract inspected; full text not inspected.

**System / methods:** Sodium silicate added to sulfuric acid to a target pH; gels then aged, washed and dried under controlled conditions. Dry-gel surface properties measured using nitrogen and nitrous-oxide adsorption isotherms and BET surface-area calculations.

**Reported evidence:** The pH range 3.7–5.8 corresponded to a large decrease in setting time, from over an hour to a few seconds. Gas adsorption isotherms changed substantially across this pH range. Residual water content and BET surface area both decreased with pH, but the authors report no simple relationship between these quantities. Varying temperature in one preparation and minimising the ageing period in another did not produce a detectable difference in nitrous-oxide adsorption under the tested conditions.

**Supports:** Preparation pH can strongly affect gelation kinetics and the final accessible surface properties of dried sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid silica gels. It is an early independent precursor-history-to-property link.

**Does not establish:** It does not directly measure wet-gel consolidation, pore-liquid expulsion or syneresis kinetics. The measured endpoint is a washed and dried gel, so drying and washing are part of the observed structure.

**Review role:** Historical background for pH-dependent gel formation and dried-product surface properties. **Class B; OBS+CALC.**

## PSG-024 — Shapiro & Kolthoff (1950), thermal ageing of precipitated silica

**Reference:** I. Shapiro and I. M. Kolthoff, “Studies on Aging of Precipitates and Coprecipitation. XLIII. Thermal Aging of Precipitated Silica (Silica Gel)”, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 72(2) (1950), 776–782. DOI: 10.1021/ja01158a035.

**Verification:** ACS publisher record confirms title, authors, journal, volume, issue, pages, date and DOI. Abstract and full text were not available in the inspected record.

**System / methods:** Explicitly identified by the title as thermal ageing of precipitated silica/silica gel. Experimental conditions and observables remain to be verified from the full article.

**What can currently be claimed:** Only that an early primary study specifically addressed thermal ageing of precipitated silica gel. No quantitative result or mechanism should be attributed until the paper is inspected.

**Review role:** High-priority retrieval target because it may provide independent historical evidence for thermal ageing of precipitated silica. **Provisional class B; verification of scientific content pending.** Do not cite for a substantive result yet.

## PSG-025 — Merrill & Spencer (1950), sodium silicate gelation

**Reference:** R. C. Merrill and R. W. Spencer, “The Gelation of Sodium Silicate. Effect of Sulfuric Acid, Hydrochloric Acid, Ammonium Sulfate, and Sodium Aluminate”, *The Journal of Physical Chemistry* 54(6) (1950), 806–812. DOI: 10.1021/j150480a009.

**Verification:** ACS publisher metadata and DOI verified. Full text is paywalled; abstract not available in the inspected record.

**System / methods:** Sodium silicate gelation in the presence of sulfuric acid, hydrochloric acid, ammonium sulfate and sodium aluminate. Exact concentrations, measurements and results have not yet been inspected.

**What can currently be claimed:** The paper is a primary experimental study of chemical conditions affecting sodium-silicate gelation. The title alone does not justify any directional claim about the effect of each additive.

**Review role:** Historical source to retrieve for electrolyte/acid effects on gelation. **Provisional class B; content appraisal pending.**

## PSG-026 — Madeley & Sing (1962), acid concentration and addition history

**Reference:** J. D. Madeley and K. S. W. Sing, “Surface properties of silica gels. IV. Influence of acid concentration in the preparation from sodium silicate and acetic acid”, *Journal of Applied Chemistry* 12(11) (1962), 494–499. DOI: 10.1002/jctb.5010121106.

**Verification:** Wiley publisher metadata and DOI verified; abstract inspected; full text not inspected.

**System / methods:** Silica gels prepared by adding sodium silicate to acetic acid or an acetic acid/sodium acetate buffer to pH 4.64. Silicate addition was rapid (1–2 min) or slow (about 30 min). Nitrogen adsorption on outgassed samples was used to calculate surface areas and pore volumes.

**Reported evidence:** Gels made by rapid addition over the tested concentration range, or from buffer, showed similar microporous surface properties. Under slow addition, surface properties depended on acid concentration; the reported surface area for the 30% acetic-acid preparation was half that of the 10% preparation. The authors suggest loss of smaller primary particles as polysilicic-acid concentration increases and pH changes slowly.

**Supports:** Addition history and acid concentration can influence the surface properties of sodium-silicate-derived silica gels, plausibly through changes in primary-particle populations.

**Does not establish:** It does not directly investigate sulfuric-acid industrial precipitation, mechanical fragmentation or wet-gel syneresis. The proposed loss of smaller particles is an interpretation, not direct observation in the abstract.

**Review role:** Qualified comparator for precursor/addition-history effects on dried-gel surface properties. **Class B; OBS+CALC, with proposed particle-loss mechanism tagged HYP.**

## Implications for the evidence synthesis

- The independent literature is older and more substantial than the modern industrial-process papers alone would suggest, especially for gelation chemistry and dried-gel surface properties.
- The early literature supports a process-history effect on final surface properties, but usually through washed/dried endpoint measurements rather than in-situ wet-gel structural observations.
- PSG-024 and PSG-025 are important retrieval priorities, but remain bibliographic leads until their full text or sufficiently detailed abstracts are inspected.
- The modern review should connect this historical surface-characterisation literature to contemporary fragmentation/syneresis work without implying that all studies examined the same material state or process route.
