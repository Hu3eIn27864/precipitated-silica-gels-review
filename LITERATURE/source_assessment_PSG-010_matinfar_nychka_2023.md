# Source assessment PSG-010 — Matinfar & Nychka (2023): sodium silicate speciation, gelation and syneresis

## Bibliographic record

Matinfar, M.; Nychka, J. A. (2023). “A review of sodium silicate solutions: Structure, gelation, and syneresis.” *Advances in Colloid and Interface Science* 322, 103036. DOI: [10.1016/j.cis.2023.103036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cis.2023.103036). Review article; electronic publication 2 November 2023, issue December 2023.

## Scope and principal synthesis

The review treats aqueous sodium silicate (waterglass) as a dynamic mixture of water, sodium cations and anionic silicate species. It emphasizes that the species distribution spans monomers, oligomers and colloidal structures, and depends strongly on silica concentration, the SiO2:Na2O ratio, pH and temperature. It surveys spectroscopic/scattering characterization, gelation mechanisms and kinetics, syneresis, and molecular-modelling work.

The authors report that more than fifty silicate structures have been identified in aqueous sodium silicate solutions. They describe 29Si NMR as the strongest available method for identifying and quantifying solution species; their review states that the major species representing up to about 85% of solution silicon have been determined. This is a review-level synthesis, not a new NMR dataset or a claim that all transient species and reaction pathways are resolved.

For syneresis, the review describes continued condensation between and within silicate particles after gel formation as a source of pore-liquid expulsion, network consolidation and shrinkage, with consequent changes in volume, porosity and internal surface area. The review also identifies molecular modelling as a route to investigate atomistic reaction events that are difficult to isolate experimentally, while noting that adequate sampling remains a practical challenge.

## Relevance to CCFSM

This is a high-value **mechanistic synthesis** for the target sodium-silicate chemistry, but it is secondary evidence. It supports the need to treat chemical speciation and gel-network evolution as coupled state variables rather than collapsing the chemistry into a single empirical rate constant.

Three modelling implications follow:

1. **Composition state must be explicit.** Initial silica concentration and SiO2:Na2O ratio, as well as pH and temperature, condition the available silicate species and therefore the gelation pathway. A kinetic parameter calibrated for one waterglass composition should not be transferred without a matched-composition argument.
2. **Post-gelation chemistry remains active.** A network variable such as bridge/condensation density may evolve after the gel point, but its relation to macroscopic contraction must be coupled to liquid transport and mechanical resistance. Chemical condensation alone does not specify the observed shrinkage rate.
3. **A multiscale validation ladder is required.** Solution speciation (preferably 29Si NMR where feasible), aggregation/network structure (scattering or microscopy), mechanical response (rheology/modulus), and macroscopic volume/exudate measurements constrain different links in the proposed mechanism. None can substitute for all the others.

## Limits and evidence classification

- **Evidence grade:** B, review-level mechanistic synthesis.
- **Direct observations:** none newly generated by this review; experimental claims must be traced to the cited primary studies before quantitative extraction.
- **Target relevance:** high for waterglass-derived silica chemistry; formulation-specific relevance must still be checked against the project's sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitation route.
- **Not established:** a universal speciation-to-syneresis constitutive law, a validated mapping from NMR species fractions to load-bearing bridge density, or a calibrated relation between atomistic condensation rates and macroscopic permeability/modulus.
- **Modelling caution:** do not turn the review's broad mechanistic narrative into numerical rate constants or universal parameter closures without primary matched-state data.
