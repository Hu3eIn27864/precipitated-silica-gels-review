# Individual source assessments — batch 04

**Assessment date:** 11 November 2026  
**Purpose:** identify structural measurements outside the Schlomach–Quarch–Wilhelm research families, with particular attention to SAXS evidence for aggregation and ageing. The two papers below are water-glass-derived aqueous silica systems. They are valuable mechanistic comparators, but should not be presented as direct replications of industrial semi-batch precipitated silica.

---

## PSG-020 — Wijnen et al. (1991), “The molecular basis of aging of aqueous silica gel”

**Reference:** P. W. J. G. Wijnen, T. P. M. Beelen, C. P. J. Rummens, H. C. P. L. Saeijs, J. W. de Haan, L. J. M. van de Ven and R. A. van Santen, “The molecular basis of aging of aqueous silica gel”, *Journal of Colloid and Interface Science* 145(1) (1991), 17–32. DOI: 10.1016/0021-9797(91)90097-R.

**Verification status:** Bibliographic metadata and DOI verified from the publisher record and TU Eindhoven research repository. Abstract inspected; full article not inspected.

**System / methods:** Aqueous silica gels prepared from water glass. Ageing investigated using small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS), solid-state NMR and nitrogen sorption (BET).

**What the abstract directly reports:** SAXS is interpreted as showing migration of active dissolved silicate species (including monomeric silicic acid) from more soluble, less dense peripheral primary-particle regions toward denser regions in aggregate cores. The work combines structural scattering with NMR and sorption rather than relying on macroscopic shrinkage alone.

**What this supports:** Dissolution/reprecipitation or transport-and-condensation of soluble silica species is a plausible route for nanoscale structural ageing in aqueous silica gels. This is important context for interpreting consolidation and syneresis: structural evolution need not be purely mechanical, and pore-liquid chemistry may couple to network reorganisation.

**What it does not establish:** It does not establish that the same rate-limiting mechanism dominates in industrially precipitated silica fragments formed by stirred acidification of sodium silicate. Nor does the abstract establish a direct quantitative relationship between SAXS-derived local restructuring and macroscopic syneresis or permeability.

**Limitations / transferability:** Water-glass-derived aqueous gel chemistry and the ageing conditions must be compared carefully with the industrial semi-batch route. The study belongs to the same Wijnen/Beelen research lineage as Wijnen et al. (1991), *Journal of Applied Crystallography*, “Silica gel from water glass: a SAXS study of the formation and ageing of fractal aggregates”. The two papers use related systems and research teams; until exact sample and dataset overlap is checked, treat them as complementary outputs from one research family rather than independent replication.

**Review role:** Mechanistic comparator for nanoscale ageing, soluble-silicate transport and structural densification. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC.** Do not promote to direct precipitated-silica evidence.

## PSG-021 — Knoblich & Gerber (2001), “Aggregation in SiO2 sols from sodium silicate solutions”

**Reference:** B. Knoblich and Th. Gerber, “Aggregation in SiO2 sols from sodium silicate solutions”, *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 283(1–3) (2001), 109–113. DOI: 10.1016/S0022-3093(01)00356-8.

**Verification status:** Bibliographic record and DOI/title pairing identified in the publisher’s ScienceDirect record. Abstract inspected; full text not inspected.

**System / methods:** SiO2 sols derived from sodium water-glass solutions by ion exchange; small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) used to characterise aggregation clusters, their size, fractal dimension and primary units.

**What the abstract directly reports:** As preparation pH changes from 2 to 5.8, gelation time decreases strongly while the aggregation-cluster fractal dimension remains near 2.2. Above pH 5.8, gelation time increases again and the reported fractal dimension decreases toward approximately 1.7.

**What this supports:** Preparation pH can strongly alter gelation kinetics without necessarily changing the measured cluster fractal dimension over the entire pH range. Beyond a threshold, both kinetics and aggregate structure can change. This cautions against treating gelation time as a unique proxy for aggregate compactness.

**What it does not establish:** The abstract does not report post-gel consolidation, natural syneresis, fragment mechanics or a direct comparison with industrial acid/silicate co-feed precipitation. The reported SAXS values describe the aggregation clusters under the studied sol-gel preparation route.

**Limitations / transferability:** Full experimental details, pH definition, ionic-strength conditions, SAXS fitting range and uncertainty are not yet inspected. Do not compare its fractal dimensions numerically with Schlomach/Kind values until length scales, fitting models and sample histories are reconciled.

**Review role:** Independent structural comparator for pH-dependent aggregation of water-glass-derived silica sols. **Provisional class B; OBS+CALC.**

---

## Evidence implications

1. **There is independent structural evidence for aqueous silica-gel ageing**, including SAXS/NMR/sorption evidence consistent with transport of soluble silica and densification within aggregates (Wijnen et al., 1991).
2. **There is independent SAXS evidence for pH-sensitive aggregation** in sodium-silicate-derived SiO2 sols (Knoblich & Gerber, 2001).
3. **The direct industrial precipitated-silica evidence remains comparatively concentrated.** These comparator studies strengthen the mechanistic discussion but do not close the external-replication gap for fragmented industrial precipitated silica.
4. **Avoid overinterpreting fractal dimension.** It depends on preparation route, pH, time, length-scale window and fitting assumptions; values from different studies should not be assembled into one trend without methodological harmonisation.
5. **Priority for the next evidence pass:** search for independent studies that measure structural evolution in actual precipitated silica powders or wet fragments under ageing/consolidation conditions, and distinguish powder ageing/hygrothermal exposure from wet-gel syneresis.
