# Source assessment — Scherer (1995): pH-dependent permeability and viscoelasticity of silica gels

**Record:** PSG-044  
**Citation:** G. W. Scherer, “Viscoelasticity and permeability of silica gels,” *Faraday Discussions* **101** (1995), 225–234. DOI: [10.1039/FD9950100225](https://doi.org/10.1039/FD9950100225).  
**Evidence class:** B — mechanistic comparator, not direct validation of industrial precipitated silica.  
**Access level:** Princeton institutional publication record and author-group bibliography inspected; original article full text not accessed.

## What the accessible abstract supports

The study reports a beam-bending method for measuring gel permeability and viscoelastic properties. It distinguishes elastic behaviour in an inert solvent (ethyl formate) from viscoelastic behaviour in aqueous solution. For buffer solutions, the reported relaxation rate increases with pH. The abstract also reports increasing permeability with pH, associated with coarsening and larger pores; pore radii are given as approximately 14–20 nm. The shear modulus decreases slightly with pH. The authors interpret these changes in terms of siloxane-bond attack and dissolution/reprecipitation, which can reduce inter-cluster connectivity while increasing intra-cluster connectivity.

## Relevance to this project

This is a useful mechanistic bridge between the evolving silica network and transport:

- Permeability is not a fixed input independent of gel chemistry or structural evolution.
- The skeleton's viscoelastic response changes with pore-liquid chemistry and pH.
- Pore coarsening, connectivity and mechanical modulus can evolve together, so a single constant consolidation coefficient cannot identify permeability by itself.
- It strengthens the rationale for a future coupled model in which network reorganisation changes both hydraulic transport and mechanical resistance.

## Limits and non-transferability

The accessible abstract does not establish the precursor route, exact gel composition, buffer identity/concentration, ionic strength, or detailed measurement protocol. It is therefore not safe to assume that these were industrial sodium-silicate/sulfuric-acid precipitated-silica gels. The approximate pore-radius range and reported pH trends must not be imported as numerical parameters for our target material.

The paper belongs to the broader Scherer silica-gel mechanics/permeability research family, alongside PSG-037 (1989 theory), PSG-038 (1989 experimental syneresis), and PSG-043 (1989 permeability measurement II). These papers are complementary but not independent external replications; shared authorship and likely overlapping methods must be accounted for.

## Evidence disposition

- Supports the qualitative claim that permeability and viscoelasticity can co-evolve with pH and silica-gel structure: **yes, as comparator evidence**.
- Supports a numerical pH-dependent permeability law for precipitated silica: **no**.
- Supports using a constant permeability or constant skeleton modulus throughout syneresis: **no**.
- Suitable for calibration of the present radial solver: **no**; it can inform future model structure, but target-system parameters and direct data are still missing.

## Verification still required

Obtain and inspect the full text to verify precursor chemistry, buffer conditions, pore-size method, beam geometry, permeability values, modulus values, relaxation spectra, uncertainty, and whether the quoted pH trend is measured over a range relevant to precipitated silica.
