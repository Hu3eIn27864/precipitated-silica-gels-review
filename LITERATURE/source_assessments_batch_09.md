## PSG-031 — Sarawade et al. (2011), drying-dependent texture of precipitated silica

**Reference:** P. B. Sarawade, J.-K. Kim, A. Hilonga, D. V. Quang and H. T. Kim, “Effect of drying technique on the physicochemical properties of sodium silicate-based mesoporous precipitated silica”, *Applied Surface Science* 258(2) (2011), 955–961. DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2011.09.035.

**Verification:** Full article PDF inspected. Bibliographic metadata and DOI are consistent with the publisher record. The PDF was accessible from an author-hosted copy; the content corresponds to the publisher article.

**Material / wet-gel preparation:** Industrial-grade sodium silicate (SiO2/Na2O molar ratio 3.4) and NaCl were used. In a 10 L reactor, 2080 g of 24 wt% sodium silicate solution was mixed with 6682 g of 4 wt% aqueous NaCl. The mixture was held at 35 °C and 300 rpm. An 8 wt% H2SO4 solution was added in two stages: 985 g at 197 g/min, followed by a 10 min hold without acid addition; a further 1580 g was then added until pH 5. The authors describe a semi-gelatic point within about one minute of the first acid addition. The wet-gel slurry was aged for 1 h at 85 °C under 150 rpm stirring, cooled, washed to remove Na+, Cl− and sulfate by-products, and filtered to a wet cake.

**Drying treatments:**
- Oven: wet cake dried at 80 °C for 4 h, then at 200 °C for 1 h.
- Microwave: ambient-pressure microwave drying at 2.45 GHz and 0.7 kW; exposure time varied.
- Spray: pilot-plant spray dryer, with reported inlet/outlet temperatures 180/100 °C and a nozzle-based atomisation process. The cake was diluted with water before spray drying.

**Direct quantitative observations (Table 1):**

| Drying method | Surface area (m²/g) | Pore diameter (nm) | Pore volume (cm³/g) | Porosity (%) | Tapping density (g/cm³) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Oven | 358 | 7.51 | 0.91 | 80.95 | 0.362 |
| Microwave | 437 | 10.21 | 1.27 | 84.52 | 0.294 |
| Spray | 679 | 17.41 | 1.96 | 88.16 | 0.225 |

The article states that results are based on three sets of experiments. It also reports a progressive increase in microwave-dried sample surface area and pore volume over 10–30 min (BET area 426→437 m²/g; pore volume 1.21→1.27 cm³/g).

**Interpretation reported by the authors:** Oven drying produces greater drying shrinkage and denser aggregates; spray drying is interpreted as reducing shrinkage and preserving a more open pore structure. Capillary forces during water removal are invoked to explain the stronger shrinkage during conventional drying.

**Supports:** Drying history alone can substantially alter the final textural properties of a precipitated-silica powder prepared from a common wet-gel slurry. Therefore, endpoint BET area, pore volume, pore size and apparent morphology cannot be attributed solely to precipitation, gelation or wet-gel syneresis unless the drying protocol is controlled.

**Does not establish:** This study does not quantify natural syneresis or wet-gel consolidation before filtration/drying. It does not compare distinct precipitation histories; it compares drying routes applied to a prepared wet-gel slurry. It therefore demonstrates a downstream confounder, not the mechanism behind Quang et al.'s gelation-time effect.

**Methodological caveats:** Table 1 labels the surface-area column “BJH method”, although the text refers to BET surface area and Table 2 explicitly labels its surface-area values as BET. Treat the Table 1 area-method label as ambiguous pending confirmation from raw data or author clarification. The paper reports three experimental sets, but the accessible text does not establish that every adsorption-derived metric has the same replicate/uncertainty treatment. Drying treatments also differ in thermal history and equipment scale. The authors' capillary-shrinkage explanation is plausible but not a direct in situ measurement of pore-scale stresses.

**Review role:** Core evidence for a distinct final-processing stage: wet-gel structure → filtration/wet cake → drying-induced shrinkage/restructuring → final powder texture. **Class A for the demonstrated sensitivity of final powder texture to drying protocol; OBS+CALC.**

---

## Synthesis implication

The combination of Quang et al. (2012) and Sarawade et al. (2011) suggests a useful review structure for final product properties:

1. Gelation and pre-gel process history can influence the structural state inherited by the wet gel and the final powder.
2. Subsequent ageing, washing and filtration create additional process history.
3. Drying can independently produce substantial changes in surface area, pore volume, pore size and packing density.

Do not collapse these stages into one generic “gel densification” process. In particular, final BET values are not direct proxies for wet-gel syneresis.
