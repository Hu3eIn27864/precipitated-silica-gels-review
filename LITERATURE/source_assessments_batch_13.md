# Source assessments — batch 13

## PSG-035 — Tognonvi, Soro & Rossignol (2012): effect of alkali-cation size

**Reference:** M. T. Tognonvi, J. Soro and S. Rossignol, “Physical-chemistry of silica/alkaline silicate interactions during consolidation. Part 1: Effect of cation size,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 358(1) (2012), 81–87. DOI: 10.1016/j.jnoncrysol.2011.08.027.

**Verification and access:** Publisher record and publication-level abstract confirm the bibliographic record. Full text was not inspected.

**System and methods:** Quartz sand (mean grain diameter 340 µm) is agglomerated using diluted lithium-, sodium- and potassium-silicate solutions at [Si] = 2.8 mol/L. Consolidation occurs during drying at 70 °C. Abstract reports drying-time measurements, compressive strength and SEM fracture observations.

**Reported findings:** Drying time increases as cation size decreases; compressive strength increases with cation size. SEM observations indicate intragranular fracture in stronger materials. Potassium-based materials are nevertheless highly water-soluble.

**Limits:** This is waterglass-bonded quartz sand during drying, not wet-gel syneresis of acid-precipitated amorphous silica. The proposed cation-hydration/bonding explanation is mechanistic interpretation, not direct molecular-scale measurement. Keep separate from direct precipitated-silica evidence.

**Research-family note:** Related to Tognonvi/Rossignol studies on alkaline silicate–silica interactions; dataset independence from companion papers has not been established.

**Role and grade:** Mechanistic comparator only; OBS;HYP; class B.

## PSG-036 — Tognonvi et al. (2012): effect of pH

**Reference:** M. T. Tognonvi, J. Soro, J.-L. Gelet and S. Rossignol, “Physico-chemistry of silica / Na silicate interactions during consolidation. Part 2: Effect of pH,” *Journal of Non-Crystalline Solids* 358(3) (2012), 492–501. DOI: 10.1016/j.jnoncrysol.2011.11.005.

**Verification and access:** Elsevier publisher record confirms the bibliographic details. Publisher abstract and extensive preview inspected; original full PDF not independently accessed.

**System and methods:** Quartz sand is impregnated with diluted commercial sodium silicate solution. The solution is further alkalinised with NaOH to pH values approximately 12.5, 13.6 and 14 (starting diluted solution around pH 11.3). Samples are consolidated during drying at 70 °C. The publisher preview reports gravimetric and ATR-FTIR monitoring, compression tests and SEM observations; some sand surfaces are also pre-functionalised by basic attack.

**Reported findings:** Higher solution pH and basic pre-treatment of the sand surface improve mechanical properties. FTIR band shifts and SEM intragranular fracture are interpreted as evidence consistent with dissolution–precipitation and stronger interfacial reaction. Increased sodium content also increases water solubility.

**Limits:** This is an alkaline waterglass/quartz-sand composite undergoing drying, not isolated precipitated-silica wet-gel syneresis. FTIR shifts and fracture patterns do not uniquely establish the full dissolution–precipitation pathway. High pH is accompanied by higher NaOH/Na+ content, so pH is not an isolated chemical variable.

**Research-family note:** Part of the Tognonvi/Soro/Gelet/Rossignol programme; related to Lucas et al. (2011), Kouassi et al. (2011), and the cation-size Part 1 paper. Do not count as independent external replication without checking preparation dates and dataset overlap.

**Role and grade:** Mechanistic comparator only; OBS;HYP; class B.

## Synthesis note

The three connected papers (Lucas 2011; Tognonvi et al. 2012 Part 1 and Part 2) form a useful parameter study of alkaline waterglass/quartz-sand consolidation during drying. They show that drying temperature, alkali-cation identity and pH influence mechanical cohesion and proposed interfacial chemistry. Their value for the review is to illustrate how silicate speciation and silica-surface reactivity can influence consolidation in a related system. They do not supply direct wet-gel syneresis evidence and must remain in a clearly labelled comparator category.
