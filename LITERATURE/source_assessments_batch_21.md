# Source assessment batch 21 — direct precipitated-silica consolidation

## PSG-003 — Sahabi & Kind (2011)

**Reference:** Sahabi, H. & Kind, M. (2011). “Consolidation of Inorganic Precipitated Silica Gel.” *Polymers* 3(3), 1423–1432. DOI: [10.3390/polym3031423](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym3031423). Open full text: https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/fa4e/b7fff95fc8b4c022845ccbe851253bb1b645.pdf

### Material and protocol
- Sodium silicate solution (40 wt%) mixed 1:1 with sulfuric-acid feed in a Y-mixer.
- Acidic model gel: 50 wt% H2SO4 feed, pH approximately -0.97.
- Alkaline model gel: 3.2 wt% H2SO4 feed, pH approximately 9.
- Short gelation time approximately 12 min.
- Oedometer compression with double drainage (top and bottom), porous filter plates and an NF90 nanofiltration membrane to retain fine gel fragments.
- Applied pressure approximately 15 bar; temperature comparisons at 20 and 80 °C.

### Reported quantitative results
Table 1 reports the fitted time to 90% consolidation, t90:
| Gel | 20 °C | 80 °C |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alkaline (pH ~9) | 40,401 s (11.22 h) | 11,236 s (3.12 h) |
| Acidic (pH -0.97) | 31,506 s (8.75 h) | 5,625 s (1.56 h) |

Thus, within this forced-consolidation protocol, heating from 20 to 80 °C reduces t90 by factors of approximately 3.60 (alkaline) and 5.60 (acidic). The acidic model gel consolidates faster than the alkaline gel at both tested temperatures. These are source-reported fitted t90 values, not independently measured permeability or modulus.

### Model and interpretation limits
The authors use a semi-empirical Terzaghi–Shirato relation:
\[
U=1-\exp(-\pi^2 T_c/4),\qquad T_c=i^2 C_e t/A^2
\]
with two drainage surfaces (i=2). They define t90 through the corresponding exponential fit. The authors explicitly report deviations between the experimental and theoretical curves during primary consolidation (except for the acidic 80 °C case) and suggest that a damping element may be needed in the equivalent mechanical network. They also report secondary-consolidation deviations.

The study is therefore especially useful for two review claims:
1. Process chemistry (acidic versus alkaline formulation) and temperature change the mechanical consolidation response of direct precipitated-silica gels.
2. A purely elastic Terzaghi-type skeleton is insufficient to describe all observed time regimes; viscoelastic/damped response is a justified model extension to test.

### Transferability and caveats
- This is direct target-chemistry evidence, but under externally forced oedometer loading at approximately 15 bar. It is not a natural-syneresis experiment.
- The NF90 membrane, filter plates, double-drainage geometry and imposed stress are part of the measured response.
- The reported t90 parameter lumps hydraulic transport, skeleton compliance/relaxation, geometry and boundary resistance. The paper does not independently report intrinsic permeability and the constrained modulus needed to separate these contributions.
- The authors qualitatively compare their acidic oedometer curve with natural syneresis data from other studies, but the chemistry, pH, pressure state and final shrinkage differ. Do not pool those curves or use their ratio as a direct natural-to-forced conversion.
- The paper reports approximately 75% consolidation in about 20 h in the example oedometer experiment, compared with approximately 15% natural syneresis over 70–150 h in cited acidic gels. This is a qualitative contrast between different protocols, not a matched quantitative comparison.

**Evidence class:** A — direct precipitated-silica chemistry; forced mechanical consolidation. No permeability measurement and no direct calibration of the current poroelastic model.
