# Source assessments — batch 15: Soviet-era polysilicic-acid hydrogel studies

## Scope and verification standard

This batch records four highly relevant historical papers associated with S. I. Kontorovich and T. P. Ponomareva. Bibliographic details are supported by reference lists in later scholarly works and accessible university/repository documents. The original 1980s/1991 journal texts have not yet been located. Therefore all four records remain workflow status **V (verification pending)**, not an evidence-quality grade. No results are inferred from titles.

## PSG-039 — Ponomareva et al. (1984): shrinkage kinetics during hydrogel formation

**Reference:** T. P. Ponomareva, S. I. Kontorovich, M. I. Chekanov and E. D. Shchukin, “Kinetics of shrinkage during formation of polysilicic acid hydrogel structures,” *Colloid Journal of the USSR* 46(1) (1984), 118–120.

**Bibliographic verification:** Cited by Wilhelm & Kind (2015), reference 15, and independently listed in secondary scholarly bibliographies. Original journal issue not located.

**Scientific relevance:** The title directly identifies shrinkage during formation of polysilicic-acid hydrogel structures. It may concern early structural contraction around gel formation rather than post-gel syneresis alone; the distinction must be established from the paper.

**Unknowns:** Precursor chemistry, pH, ionic composition, hydrogel concentration, experimental geometry, shrinkage observable, time resolution and results are all unverified.

**Role:** Priority retrieval target; do not use for quantitative or mechanistic claims until original text is inspected. Status V.

## PSG-040 — Kontorovich et al. (1988): temperature dependence of syneresis

**Reference:** S. I. Kontorovich, T. P. Ponomareva and L. N. Sokolova, “Influence of temperature on the kinetics of syneresis of acidic and alkaline polysilicic acid hydrogels,” *Colloid Journal of the USSR* 50(6) (1988), 1100–1104.

**Bibliographic verification:** Authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue and pages agree across two secondary scholarly bibliographies. Original journal text not located.

**Scientific relevance:** This is a particularly promising direct comparator because the title explicitly identifies acidic and alkaline polysilicic-acid hydrogels and syneresis kinetics as the variables of interest.

**Unknowns:** Do not infer whether increasing temperature accelerates syneresis, changes the final contraction, or affects acid and alkaline gels differently. Exact preparation routes, compositions and measurement method remain unknown.

**Role:** Priority retrieval target for comparison with Wilhelm & Kind (2015). Status V.

## PSG-041 — Kontorovich et al. (1988): elastic modulus during syneresis

**Reference:** S. I. Kontorovich, T. P. Ponomareva and L. N. Sokolova, “Change in the elastic modulus of polysilicic-acid hydrogels during syneresis,” *Colloid Journal of the USSR* 50(5) (1988), 1010–1011.

**Bibliographic verification:** Bibliographic details are supported by a scholarly bibliography and a university repository reference list. Original journal text not located.

**Scientific relevance:** Potentially the most valuable bridge between macroscopic syneresis and mechanical evolution, because it explicitly addresses modulus during syneresis. This is a short communication (two pages), so method detail may be limited.

**Unknowns:** The title does not reveal whether modulus rises or falls, which modulus definition or instrument was used, or whether the measured change is reversible. No direction or magnitude is asserted.

**Role:** Priority retrieval target; potentially central to the review's structure–mechanics coupling section if methods and material system prove relevant. Status V.

## PSG-042 — Kontorovich et al. (1991): electrolytes, polycondensation and syneresis

**Reference:** S. I. Kontorovich, L. N. Sokolova, E. A. Golubeva, E. I. Kochetkova, N. P. Sokolova and E. D. Shchukin, “On the influence of electrolytes on the polycondensation of silicic acids and the syneresis process,” *Colloid Journal of the USSR* 53(1) (1991), provisionally 126–128.

**Bibliographic verification:** Multiple secondary bibliographies list the paper. Most give pages 126–128; one gives 126–129. The pagination must be checked against the original issue.

**Scientific relevance:** Directly addresses electrolyte effects on both silicic-acid polycondensation and syneresis, potentially helping distinguish changes in colloidal stability/aggregation from post-gel restructuring.

**Unknowns:** Electrolyte identities and concentrations, ionic strength, pH, whether the system is a hydrosol or formed hydrogel, measured endpoints and results are unverified.

**Role:** Priority retrieval target; no salt-specific mechanism or direction of effect should be cited yet. Status V.

## Research-family interpretation and next steps

These four papers form a coherent Kontorovich–Ponomareva–Sokolova research line. They should not be counted as four independent research programmes. The 1988 temperature and modulus papers share authors and year; the 1984 and 1991 papers include overlapping investigators. Dataset overlap and continuity of sample preparation remain unknown.

A secondary synthesis by Neimark & Sheinfain (1973), *Silica Gel: Its Preparation, Properties and Application*, provides valuable historical context on hydrogel ageing, pH, electrolyte treatment and pore-structure changes, but it predates these papers and is not a substitute for their primary data. It also explicitly notes that older electrolyte studies often used non-comparable hydrogel preparation, treatment and drying conditions, limiting cross-study interpretation.

**Retrieval priority:** (1) 1988 modulus paper; (2) 1988 acid/base temperature kinetics; (3) 1991 electrolyte/syneresis paper; (4) 1984 shrinkage-kinetics paper. Search Russian-language library catalogues, former USSR journal archives, institutional repositories and interlibrary loan. Until full texts are obtained, these records remain excluded from quantitative synthesis.
